## 1. What do the systems' ablations report: quality with vs. without the LLM planner, and which planning component gives the largest gain?

### Takeaway
With the video model held fixed, removing or weakening the LLM planning layer costs roughly 0.2–0.8 points on 5-point human/LLM-judge scales (about 5–20 % relative) in the papers where it is measured; the largest single drops come from removing the shot-decomposition / storyboard step (ViMax: 4.107 → 3.658 average, one sub-score 4.94 → 3.10) and from removing visual anchors (reference/keyframe conditioning), not from prompt-wording refinements. Almost all of these numbers are self-reported, small-sample, and judged partly by LLMs.

### Inferences
- Reading across papers, the ordering of effect sizes when the renderer is fixed is roughly: (a) visual anchoring planned by the agent — reference images, keyframes, start/end frames (VideoStudio +34.5 over a text-only planner; "Lights, Camera, Consistency" 7.99 vs 0.55 [2nd]; OSOD first-frame ablation hits continuity hardest) ≥ (b) shot decomposition / storyboard (ViMax −0.45 average, −1.84 on one sub-score; Script paper's segmentation + anchoring) > (c) multi-candidate review and selection (AniMaker −7.1 %; ViMax −3.8 % or −0.24) > (d) richer per-shot script/prompt wording alone (third-party estimate 0.1–0.2 points in the Script paper; ViMax RAG for camera language ±0.00). This supports "allocation/storyboarding and anchoring matter more than prompt wording", but it is an inference from heterogeneous, mostly self-reported ablations, not a single controlled study.
- The LLM's identity matters about as much as the pipeline structure in the one paper that varies both: MovieAgent's spread across LLMs (3.44–3.82) is about 0.38 points, vs 0.27 points between the no-CoT/no-multi-agent and full configurations with GPT-4o. AnimeAgent's 2B-vs-30B Director result points the same way.
- LVD's 98 % plan accuracy vs 49.4 % rendered accuracy is the clearest (if old) demonstration that a correct plan is necessary but its value is capped by what the video model can execute.

### Gaps
- No full ablation tables were read (arXiv blocked). Exact cells for VideoDirectorGPT (Tables 3, 6, Appendix H.1), VGoT (Table 3, Fig. 8), CoAgent, STAGE, MVAgent, OSOD, StoryEngine, FilmWorld (arXiv 2607.19038), CineEvolve (arXiv 2610.29590), Captain Cinema (arXiv 2507.18634) and MovieDreamer (arXiv 2407.16655) are missing.
- Nothing usable was found for DirecT2V, Vlogger, Mora, GPT4Motion, DreamFactory or Anim-Director's own ablations (Anim-Director is reported only as "ablations verify its components").
- No paper found uses MiniMax H3 or Seedance as the fixed backbone; nearest backbones in the evidence are Wan2.6, HYVideo1.5, Veo 3.1, Sora2-Pro.
- No independent replication of any of these ablations was found; every "with vs. without planner" number is from the system's own authors.

## 2. Hierarchical planning (script → scene → shot) vs. single-pass prompting; multi-agent role separation vs. a single agent

### Takeaway
The measured evidence favours staged planning over one-pass prompt generation and role-separated agents with critique over one agent, but the samples are tiny (15 story ideas; 3 movies) and the gains are moderate (about +0.2 to +0.9 on 5-point scales). Role separation appears to help mainly because it introduces review/verification steps, not because of the roles as such.

### Cited Findings
- *FilmAgent: A Multi-Agent Framework for End-to-End Film Automation in Virtual 3D Spaces* (arXiv 2501.12909; renders in Unity 3D spaces, so rendering is deterministic and only planning varies). Measured: human evaluation over 15 story ideas on four aspects — full multi-agent ("Group") FilmAgent averages 3.98 / 5; the single-agent "Solo" variant (one agent does planning, scriptwriting and cinematography without the collaboration algorithms) is reported at 3.04 [2nd for 3.04]. Claim: GPT-4o-based multi-agent FilmAgent outperforms a single-agent o1 overall, while an o1 chain-of-thought agent beats the GPT-4o chain-of-thought baseline and beats single-agent FilmAgent on some aspects. Collaboration strategies are Critique-Correct-Verify and Debate-Judge; no per-strategy ablation numbers were found. — [arXiv 2501.12909v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12909v1); [project page](https://filmagent.github.io/); [emergentmind](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2501.12909)
- MovieAgent (arXiv 2503.07314), measured with GPT-4o: multi-agent alone +0.17 average (3.55 → 3.72) and +0.33 script faithfulness; internal CoT alone +0.06 average; both +0.27 average, +0.56 script faithfulness, +0.46 narrative coherence. Authors' reading: multi-agent gains are the larger of the two. [P, mapping caveat as in §1] — [arXiv 2503.07314](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.07314)
- ViMax (arXiv 2606.07649): replacing the StoryboardArtist agent with deterministic screenplay segmentation (i.e. no LLM judgement about how to cut the scene into shots) lowers the average from 4.107 to 3.658 and VSU from 4.94 to 3.10. — [arXiv 2606.07649](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07649)
- The Script is All You Need (arXiv 2601.17737): the four-level ablation (raw dialogue → script → script + shot-aware segmentation → + frame anchoring) improves monotonically, with shot-aware segmentation + anchoring credited for pacing and camera articulation. — [arXiv 2601.17737](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17737)
- MAViS (arXiv 2508.08487): removing the Structure Reviewer (the reviewer of narrative structure at script stage) collapses the Narrative score from 34.00 to 13.67 — by far the largest of its three reviewer ablations. — [arXiv 2508.08487v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08487v2)
- *CineEvolve: An Agentic Self-Evolution Framework for Cinematic Multi-Shot Video Generation* (arXiv 2610.29590, Oct 2026): uses a **single** vision-language policy acting as both Director and Critic, trained on CineTraj (about 40K trajectories of creation, critique and revision); authors criticise prior multi-agent work as "feed-forward workflows without policy updates". Claimed to outperform existing approaches; numbers not retrieved. — [arXiv 2610.29590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29590)
- *SAGE: Self-Evolving Storyboard Skills via Attribution-Guided Rule Evolution* (arXiv 2608.17468, Aug 2026): derives storyboard rules by contrasting screenplays with expert storyboards and compares camera-language choices against Few-shot, CoT and Vanilla prompting baselines; metrics not retrieved. — [arXiv 2608.17468](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17468)
- *CineForge: Self-Improving Agents for Long-Horizon Video Generation* (arXiv 2608.29621, Aug 2026): cross-story policy evolution gives an 8.8 % relative improvement over Round 0 (4.024) [?: metric not named]. — [arXiv 2608.29621](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.29621)
- VISTA (arXiv 2510.15831) reports single-scene and multi-scene results separately; its gain over direct prompting is about the same in both (45.9 % vs 46.3 % win rate at iteration 5; see §3). — [arXiv 2510.15831v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15831v1)

### Inferences
- In both papers that isolate it (MovieAgent, FilmAgent), the multi-agent gain is larger than the gain from simply asking one model to reason step by step, and FilmAgent's "weaker model in a critique structure beats stronger model alone" claim suggests structure can substitute for some model strength. But FilmAgent's roles come bundled with Critique-Correct-Verify loops, so the result is equally consistent with "review steps matter" rather than "separate personas matter".
- 2026 work (CineEvolve, MVAgent, SAGE, CineForge) is moving away from hand-written multi-role prompt pipelines toward one planning policy that is trained or evolves from rendered feedback — an implicit admission that static role-play pipelines plateau.
- The MAViS Structure-Reviewer result and the ViMax StoryboardArtist result both say the early, structural decisions (story structure, shot breakdown) carry more of the outcome than late-stage polishing.

### Gaps
- No study found that compares hierarchical vs single-pass planning with a strong 2026 LLM where single-pass has a long context and the same information; older baselines for "single pass" are weak.
- No per-strategy ablation numbers for FilmAgent (Critique-Correct-Verify vs Debate-Judge), and the o1 single-agent average was not confirmed.
- DreamFactory (arXiv 2408.11788): no ablation evidence found.
- No paper found tests Claude models specifically as the director/planner in these pipelines.

## 3. LLM/VLM-in-the-loop review and regeneration (critic loops, take selection): how large is the effect vs. planning?

### Takeaway
Review-and-regenerate loops give consistent, measurable gains — typically 4–10 % relative on composite scores, or a 46–66 % preference/win rate over one-shot prompting after about 5 iterations — and where both are ablated in the same paper, the review loop is worth about half as much as the storyboard/planning step. Reviewer loops can also bias results toward static footage.

### Cited Findings
- *VISTA: A Test-Time Self-Improving Video Generation Agent* (Google; arXiv 2510.15831, CVPR 2026). Loop: generate candidates → pairwise tournament to pick the best clip → visual, audio and context critics → reasoning agent rewrites the prompt; no model weights are updated. Measured: win rate against direct prompting rises with iterations, reaching 45.9 % (single-scene) and 46.3 % (multi-scene) at iteration 5; win rates against baselines range 27.8–60.0 % (single-scene) and 18.5–53.2 % (multi-scene); human raters preferred VISTA's output in 66.4 % of comparisons; cost about 0.7 million tokens per iteration, excluding generation. Gains are not uniform across video models. (The tie and loss shares were not retrieved; if the comparison allows ties, as the separate "win rate" wording suggests, 46 % should not be read as "below half" — unverified.) [P] — [arXiv 2510.15831v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15831v1); [CVF PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/papers/Long_VISTA_A_Test-Time_Self-Improving_Video_Generation_Agent_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf); [project page](https://g-vista.github.io/)
- AniMaker (arXiv 2506.10540): generating one candidate per clip instead of MCTS-Gen multi-candidate search costs about 7.1 %; choosing among candidates with VBench instead of the purpose-built AniEval costs about 4.6 % — i.e. the quality of the selector is worth about two thirds of the value of having candidates at all. [2nd] — [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/animaker-automated-multi-agent-animated-storytelling-with-mcts-driven-clip-generation); [alphaXiv overview](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2506.10540)
- ViMax (arXiv 2606.07649): dropping VLM-based candidate selection lowers global consistency by 3.8 % (v1); in the later ablation "w/o PC" averages 3.863 vs 4.107 full, against 3.658 without the StoryboardArtist. — [arXiv 2606.07649v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07649v1); [arXiv 2606.07649](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07649)
- MAViS (arXiv 2508.08487): reviewers at the script stage — Narrative 34.00 full vs 13.67 / 32.33 / 20.00 without Structure / Content / Style reviewer. The 3E review principle lowers Dynamic Degree in I2V (35.62 with vs 44.58 without). [P] — [arXiv 2508.08487v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08487v2)
- *GenMAC: Compositional Text-to-Video Generation with Multi-Agent Collaboration* (arXiv 2412.04440, AAAI): iterative verify-and-refine loop; from iteration 1 to 9 the improvements are 40 %, 31 % and 31 % for numeracy, spatial relationships and motion binding; corrected ratio rises with iterations. [P] — [arXiv 2412.04440](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.04440)
- *VideoRepair* / *Self-Correcting Text-to-Video Generation with Misalignment Detection and Localized Refinement* (arXiv 2411.15115, ACL 2026 Findings): training-free, MLLM detects misalignment, plans what to keep, regenerates locally; improves over the base model on EvalCrafter and T2V-CompBench across four backbones (T2V-turbo, VideoCrafter2, CogVideoX-5B, Wan2.1-1.3B); per-metric numbers not legible. — [arXiv 2411.15115](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15115); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1817/)
- Co-Director (arXiv 2604.24842): removing the local multimodal self-refinement loops causes "a major drop in visual consistency" (qualitative in search text). — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.24842)
- OSOD (arXiv 2605.22144): removing multi-stage review degrades every metric (numbers not retrieved). — [arXiv 2605.22144v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22144v1)
- FilMaster (arXiv 2506.18899): the post-production stage that revises the rough cut with simulated audience feedback accounts for 4.17 → 4.67, more than the camera-language module's 3.75 → 4.17 [2nd]. — [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/filmaster-bridging-cinematic-principles-and-generative-ai-for-automated-film-generation)
- Limitation, CoAgent (arXiv 2512.22536): the Verifier checks keyframes rather than every frame to save compute, so high-frequency glitches can persist [2nd]. — [arXiv 2512.22536](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.22536)
- Limitation, AniMaker: VBench "favors static consistency and handles multi-scene animation poorly", which is why the authors built a context-aware evaluator that scores each clip against its neighbours. [P] — [arXiv 2506.10540](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.10540)

### Inferences
- In-paper comparison of planning vs review: ViMax — storyboard agent −10.9 % ((4.107−3.658)/4.107) vs "PC" −5.9 % ((4.107−3.863)/4.107); AniMaker — candidate search −7.1 %. Review/selection is real value, roughly half to two thirds of the planning effect, and it multiplies generation cost (several takes per shot), whereas planning is nearly free in GPU terms.
- A reviewer that only sees stills/keyframes, or that scores with metrics rewarding consistency, will select calmer, more static takes (MAViS Dynamic Degree 44.58 → 35.62; AniMaker's VBench remark; CoAgent's keyframe-only verifier). For a studio whose reviewer "sees stills, never motion", this is the specific bias to expect.
- Where the review happens matters: MAViS's largest effect is a reviewer of story structure before anything is rendered; FilMaster's largest is review of the assembled cut. Take-level selection is the smallest of the three in the papers that separate them.
- VISTA shows diminishing but still positive returns out to 5 iterations and model-dependent gains, so the benefit of prompt-rewrite loops depends on how well the fixed video model follows fine prompt detail.

### Gaps
- No paper found that reports the gain of take selection as a function of the number of takes per shot (e.g. best-of-2 vs best-of-4) on a current commercial video model.
- VISTA's tie/loss shares and per-video-model tables were not retrieved; a secondary claim that the win rate climbs "from 13 %" at the first iteration is unverified.
- No human-vs-VLM reviewer agreement figures for video takes beyond FilmEval's 0.6230 correlation and ViStoryBench's r ≈ 0.93 (image-level, [2nd]).

## 4. Which failures remain after good planning (limits set by the video model)?

### Takeaway
The benchmarks and limitation sections agree: once the plan is good, the residual errors are cross-shot continuity (identity, wardrobe, spatial layout, state), multi-event completion inside one clip, camera-instruction fidelity, and fine human performance (emotion arc, micro-expression) — areas where prompt fidelity and single-shot image quality are no longer the weak points.

### Cited Findings
- LVD (arXiv 2309.17444, 2023/ICLR 2024; old): LLM layouts 98 % correct, rendered video alignment 49.4 % — "the DSL-grounded video generation stage remains a bottleneck"; also LLMs "can still fail when generating layouts for long-horizon dynamics". — [arXiv 2309.17444v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17444v3)
- StoryEval — *Is Your World Simulator a Good Story Presenter? A Consecutive Events-Based Benchmark for Future Long Video Generation* (arXiv 2412.16211, CVPR 2025): 423 prompts, each a story of 2–4 consecutive events; none of 11 evaluated models, including the best commercial one, exceeded a 50 % average story-completion rate. (2024-era models; possibly outdated.) — [arXiv 2412.16211](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.16211); [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Wang_Is_Your_World_Simulator_a_Good_Story_Presenter_A_Consecutive_CVPR_2025_paper.html)
- *DirectorBench: Diagnosing Long-Form Video Generation with Personalized Multi-Agent Evaluation* (arXiv 2605.30090, May 2026, ByteDance + CityU HK): human raters flagged cross-shot continuity, segment-level coherence and multi-modal consistency as the weakest aspects, while prompt fidelity and local visual quality fared better; the benchmark "reveals between-unit bottlenecks" and workflow-specific failure signatures. — [arXiv 2605.30090v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30090v1)
- *MSVBench: Towards Human-Level Evaluation of Multi-Shot Video Generation* (arXiv 2602.23969, ACL 2026 Findings): 20 methods incl. commercial and agentic; finding: current systems keep strong prompt alignment but struggle with long-form stability and cross-shot consistency, "behaving more as visual interpolators than true world models". — [arXiv 2602.23969v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23969v1); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1203/)
- *PersonaShot: Benchmarking Person-Centric Narrative Continuity in Multi-Shot Video Generation* (arXiv 2608.16717, Aug 2026): about 1,000 segments, 16 metrics; reports a clear gap between perceptual quality and cross-shot narrative continuity, with micro-expression persistence and emotional-arc coherence as the main bottlenecks. — [arXiv 2608.16717v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.16717v1)
- *Stable Cinemetrics: Structured Taxonomy and Evaluation for Professional Video Generation* (arXiv 2509.26555, Stability AI): 76 control nodes in four taxonomies (Setup, Event, Lighting, Camera); 10+ models, 20K videos, 80+ film professionals as annotators; "even the strongest current models exhibit significant gaps, particularly in Events and Camera-related controls". — [arXiv 2509.26555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26555); [Stability AI](https://stability.ai/research/stable-cinemetrics-structured-taxonomy-and-evaluation-for-professional-video-generation)
- *A Survey on Long-Video Storytelling Generation: Architectures, Consistency, and Cinematic Quality* (arXiv 2507.07202, mid-2025): multi-subject long videos still fail to preserve character consistency and motion coherence; camera-relative character motion alters scale and can disrupt continuity; abrupt scene transitions hinder coherent narrative; open long-form datasets are scarce. — [arXiv 2507.07202](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07202)
- ViMax (arXiv 2606.07649), claim: the fundamental difficulty of multi-shot generation "arises from the independent nature of individual generation processes". — [arXiv 2606.07649](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07649)
- *MovieBench: A Hierarchical Movie Level Dataset for Long Video Generation* (arXiv 2411.15262, CVPR 2025): experiments surface character ID consistency, multi-view character ID coherence and audio-synchronised generation as open challenges. — [arXiv 2411.15262](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15262)
- ViStoryBench (arXiv 2505.24862): a copy-paste baseline gets near-perfect identity metrics (CIDS ≈ 96, CSD self-similarity ≈ 98, OCCM ≈ 100) but fails alignment (≈ 26) — identity consistency and narrative correctness trade off and neither implies the other [2nd]. — [emergentmind topic page](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/vistorybench)
- Seedance 2.0 (opinion/anecdote, vendor and practitioner posts): native multi-shot via timeline-style prompts, capped at 15 seconds per generation; a tester reports that very explicit multi-shot scripts under 8 seconds "tend to either flatten into a single shot or fail outright". Not controlled tests. — [PixVerse review](https://pixverse.ai/en/blog/seedance-2-0-review-prompts-and-use-cases); [Segmind tests](https://blog.segmind.com/seedance-2-0-generated-video-examples-from-real-prompt-tests/)
- Co-Director (arXiv 2604.24842): an agent pipeline on top of a video model scored 3.96 vs 3.71 for raw Veo 3.1, while older agent pipelines (MovieAgent 3.22, AniMaker 3.07) scored below raw Veo 3.1 in the same study — an agentic wrapper on a weaker renderer loses to a strong renderer with no wrapper. — [arXiv 2604.24842](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24842)

### Inferences
- The Co-Director table gives a rough sense of proportion: the best agent layer added about +0.25 (on 1–5) over the same-generation video model alone, while the gap between old-renderer agent systems and the new raw model was 0.5–0.65. Renderer generation still outweighs the agent layer; the agent layer is a second-order but reliable gain on top.
- Since the remaining failures are between-shot (continuity, state, identity), the agent's leverage on them is indirect: choosing what each shot is conditioned on (references, end frames, state carried from the previous shot — MVAgent's Observer/Transition agents, StoryEngine's start/end states) rather than wording.
- StoryEval's < 50 % multi-event completion supports giving one clip few events; that is a planning decision (shot allocation) made necessary by a model limit.

### Gaps
- No controlled evidence on H3 or Seedance 2.0 specifically; the Seedance 2.0 technical report (arXiv 2604.14148) was not retrieved.
- No head-to-head evaluation found of native multi-shot models (Seedance 2.0, HoloCine arXiv 2510.20822) against keyframe/storyboard agent pipelines on the same benchmark.
- Physics/motion failure rates conditional on a correct plan are not quantified in any source found.

## 5. Benchmarks / human evaluations of narrative coherence and cinematic quality of long multi-shot AI video — do any separate planning quality from rendering quality?

### Takeaway
No benchmark found crosses planners with renderers factorially. Several fix the plan (script, shot list, references are inputs) and so measure rendering only — ViStoryBench, MSVBench, MovieBench — while system-level benchmarks (FilmEval, DirectorBench, ViMax-Bench) score the whole pipeline; the separation has to be inferred from ablations within single papers.

### Cited Findings
- MSVBench (arXiv 2602.23969, ACL 2026 Findings): built on hierarchical scripts and reference images as inputs; 386 samples, 20+ fine-grained dimensions covering visual quality, story-video alignment and cross-shot consistency; 20 methods including agent frameworks. Human preference averages seen: AniMaker 3.17, VideoGen-of-Thought 2.07. — [arXiv 2602.23969v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23969v1); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/408321670_MSVBench_Towards_Human-Level_Evaluation_of_Multi-Shot_Video_Generation)
- *MSAVBench: Towards Comprehensive and Reliable Evaluation of Multi-Shot Audio-Video Generation* (arXiv 2605.20183): "modular" pipelines that combine separate image and audio-video tools can often outperform single end-to-end open-source models; authors suggest open-source work may benefit from agentic systems coordinating specialised tools. — [arXiv 2605.20183](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20183)
- *ViStoryBench: Comprehensive Benchmark Suite for Story Visualization* (arXiv 2505.24862): evaluates image-level story visualization from given shot scripts and character references. Findings [2nd]: GPT-4o has the highest Prompt Alignment (3.67 / 4) and OCCM (93.5 %); LLM-backed commercial models (GPT-4o, Doubao) excel in alignment but lag on low-level visual quality (Inception Score 9.02, Aesthetic 5.49); OmniGen2 leads character consistency (0.537 self-CIDS); VLM-based automatic scores correlate with human ratings at Pearson r ≈ 0.93. — [alphaXiv overview](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2505.24862v3); [emergentmind](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/vistorybench); [liner](https://liner.com/review/vistorybench-comprehensive-benchmark-suite-for-story-visualization)
- DirectorBench (arXiv 2605.30090): 80 structured metadata entries, 7 user profiles, 40 checkpoint criteria across script, visual, audio, cross-modal and stability dimensions; validated with human evaluation; reports user-dependent quality variation. Scores are not split into planning and rendering stages (per search text). — [arXiv 2605.30090](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30090)
- FilmEval (from FilMaster, arXiv 2506.18899): 12 criteria; LLM judge Gemini-1.5-Flash, average correlation with human ratings 0.6230. — [arXiv 2506.18899](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.18899)
- AniEval (from AniMaker, arXiv 2506.10540): context-aware, scores each clip against preceding and following clips; proposed because VBench is unsuitable for multi-shot animation. — [arXiv 2506.10540](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.10540)
- MovieBench (arXiv 2411.15262, CVPR 2025): 91 movies, average 45.6 minutes, annotations at movie / scene / shot level for script-to-movie generation. (One secondary source gives 116.8 hours, inconsistent with the paper's summary.) — [arXiv 2411.15262](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15262); [GitHub](https://github.com/showlab/MovieBench)
- StoryEval (arXiv 2412.16211): event-completion verified by VLMs (GPT-4o and LLaVA-OV-Chat-72B per the arXiv abstract). — [arXiv 2412.16211v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.16211v1)
- Stable Cinemetrics (arXiv 2509.26555): per-control-node questions let each cinematographic control be judged independently; a VLM evaluator aligned to expert annotations outperforms zero-shot baselines. — [Hugging Face paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2509.26555)
- Also surfaced, results not retrieved: *EntityBench* (arXiv 2605.15199; 140 episodes of up to 50 shots), *MuSS* (arXiv 2604.23789), *ShotVerse-Bench*, ViMax-Bench (35 stories). — [arXiv 2605.15199](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15199); [arXiv 2604.23789](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23789)
- Closest things to a planner × renderer design: the Script paper (same agent scripts fed to Wan2.6, HYVideo1.5, Sora2-Pro, Veo3.1, with and without the agent); MovieAgent Table 3 (four LLMs × fixed renderer); StoryEngine (one agent × two backbones); AnimeAgent (Director size 2B vs 30B × fixed I2V). — [arXiv 2601.17737v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17737v2); [arXiv 2503.07314](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.07314); [arXiv 2609.33627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33627); [arXiv 2602.20664](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20664v1)

### Inferences
- Because the fixed-plan benchmarks (MSVBench, ViStoryBench) still show large spreads between renderers and persistent cross-shot failures, a good plan clearly does not equalise outcomes; conversely the within-paper ablations (§1) show a fixed renderer's score moves 5–20 % with the plan. Both layers matter; neither benchmark type quantifies the split directly.
- LLM-judge metrics dominate this literature (FilmEval r = 0.6230 with humans; three LLM critics in the Script paper). Scores for "narrative coherence" or "camera articulation" judged by an LLM from sampled frames likely over-reward what an LLM planner writes, which inflates measured planner effects.

### Gaps
- VBench-Long: no relevant result found in searches.
- ViStoryBench dataset size and whether video (not image) methods are scored were not confirmed from a primary source in this session.
- No benchmark found that holds the renderer fixed and scores different LLM planners (GPT / Gemini / Claude / open models) on the same stories with human raters, beyond MovieAgent's 3-movie table.
- No industry blog (Higgsfield, Runway, Google Flow, ByteDance, MiniMax, Kling) with concrete measured evidence on the planning layer was found; vendor material surfaced was promotional or anecdotal.

## 6. How capable are current LLMs/VLMs at cinematography reasoning itself?

### Takeaway
On recognition benchmarks, general VLMs through 2025 were mediocre: the best model on ShotBench averaged 59.3 %, the best on CineTechBench 69.67 %, with camera movement the weakest dimension (most models under 40 % on ShotBench, at or below chance on CameraBench VQA). Specialised fine-tuned models close much of the gap by 2026. These benchmarks test recognising a technique in footage, not choosing the right technique for a story beat, which no benchmark found measures directly.

### Cited Findings
- *ShotBench: Expert-Level Cinematic Understanding in Vision-Language Models* (arXiv 2506.21356, Jun 2025): over 3.5k expert-annotated QA pairs across eight dimensions (shot size, framing, camera angle, lens size, lighting type, lighting condition, composition, camera movement). Measured: best model GPT-4o at 59.3 % average; half of the evaluated models below 50 %; more than half of the models do not exceed 40 % on camera movement; chance is 25 % (four-way multiple choice). Summary claim: all models are weak on camera movement, lens size and composition. — [arXiv 2506.21356v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21356v1); [GitHub](https://github.com/Vchitect/ShotBench); [alphaXiv overview](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2506.21356)
- *CineTechBench: A Benchmark for Cinematographic Technique Understanding and Generation* (arXiv 2505.15145, May 2025): seven dimensions (shot scale, shot angle, composition, camera movement, lighting, color, focal length); 120 movie clips and 600+ film images; 15+ MLLMs and 5+ video generation models evaluated. Gemini-2.5-Pro best overall at 69.67 %, camera-movement understanding 56.69 % [2nd]. — [arXiv 2505.15145v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15145v1); [alphaXiv overview](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2505.15145v1)
- *RefineShot: Rethinking Cinematography Understanding with Foundational Skill Evaluation* (arXiv 2510.02423, Oct 2025): rebuilds ShotBench's option sets (consistent granularity, unified dimensions, mutual exclusivity) and argues high accuracy can mask failures of reasoning consistency and instruction following; after refinement, per-class accuracy ranges from 97.3 % (e.g. "Overcast") to 0.0 % ("LED"). — [arXiv 2510.02423v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02423v1)
- *CameraBench: Towards Understanding Camera Motions in Any Video* (arXiv 2504.15376, NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight): VLMs struggle with geometric primitives that need precise trajectory estimation (SfM models struggle with semantic ones); in the VQA evaluation most VLMs perform at or below chance; Qwen2.5-VL fine-tuned on about 1,400 annotated videos writes more accurate camera-motion captions than GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Pro. — [project page](https://linzhiqiu.github.io/papers/camerabench/); [NeurIPS PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/c2d7b739f61ffbea729dad2cf9ec8c60-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf)
- *Geometry-Guided Camera Motion Understanding in VideoLLMs* (arXiv 2603.13119, Mar 2026): most off-the-shelf VideoLLMs are near the 25 % random-guess rate on its multiple-choice camera-motion benchmark. — [arXiv 2603.13119](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13119)
- *When Left Is Right? Camera-Motion Understanding beyond Screen* (CameraVLM; arXiv 2610.17242, Oct 2026): a 9B specialised model, evaluated on CameraBench, CineTechBench and ShotBench, is reported to beat all open-source and camera-motion-specific baselines by a large margin, exceed human average accuracy, and be comparable to the strongest proprietary model it tests (named "GPT-6" in the search text; exact numbers not retrieved). — [arXiv 2610.17242v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17242v1)
- Generation side: Stable Cinemetrics finds video models' largest gaps in Events and Camera controls; CineTechBench also evaluates 5+ video generators on reproducing camera movements (results not retrieved). — [arXiv 2509.26555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26555); [arXiv 2505.15145](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.15145)
- Planning side: FilMaster does not rely on the LLM's own camera sense — it retrieves camera language from 440,000 real film clips, per scene; its camera module accounts for 3.75 → 4.17 [2nd]. ViMax's analogous RAG component had no measurable effect (4.110 without vs 4.107 with). SAGE learns storyboard rules from expert storyboards rather than trusting few-shot/CoT prompting. — [arXiv 2506.18899](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.18899); [arXiv 2606.07649](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07649); [arXiv 2608.17468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17468)

### Inferences
- Two different abilities are being conflated in the question: (i) seeing — identifying shot size, lens, lighting, camera move in footage, which is what an agent needs to review takes; and (ii) writing — choosing coverage for a scene, which is text-only film-grammar knowledge. The benchmarks measure (i). Weak (i) on camera movement means a VLM reviewer is unreliable at confirming that a requested camera move actually happened, especially from stills.
- The conflicting RAG results (FilMaster helps, ViMax neutral) suggest that by 2026 strong LLMs' text-side camera-language knowledge is adequate for writing shot specs, and that the binding constraint is the video model's camera-control fidelity (Stable Cinemetrics) plus the reviewer's ability to verify it.
- 2025 scores are probably already outdated for frontier models; the 2026 CameraVLM result implies the perception gap is closable with fine-tuning, but no 2026 ShotBench/CineTechBench score for current general-purpose models (including Claude) was found.

### Gaps
- Per-dimension ShotBench and CineTechBench numbers by model (and any Claude result) were not retrieved; the CineTechBench 69.67 % / 56.69 % figures are from a secondary summary.
- *Natural Language Camera Movement Understanding* (arXiv 2607.03043): surfaced but no findings retrieved.
- No benchmark found that scores an LLM's shot-list or coverage choices against professional storyboards with human raters (SAGE compares against expert storyboards, metrics not retrieved).
