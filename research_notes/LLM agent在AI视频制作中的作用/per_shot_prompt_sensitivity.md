## 1. Measured effect of LLM prompt rewriting / enhancement / extension on video quality

### Takeaway
Rewriting a short user prompt into the dense caption style the model was trained on gives a real, repeatedly measured gain, but it is moderate on short clips (single-digit to low-double-digit points; roughly +11 to +19 preference points on 5-15 s clips in the largest 2026 human study) and it comes from *matching the training-caption distribution*, not from length as such: naive GPT-4 rewriting scored below no rewriting in one paper, and a generic refiner scored below baseline in another. Closed-loop methods that look at the output and revise the prompt beat both a fixed prompt and best-of-N seed search.

### Cited Findings
- **[M] VPO (ICCV 2025).** On CogVideoX, human pairwise evaluation: VPO "improves the win rate of 37.5% over original user queries and 14% over the official prompt optimization method" (the official one is GLM-4 in-context rewriting; GPT-4o in-context is also a baseline). Table 1 cell values (VBench, MonetBench) were not retrievable. — [arXiv 2503.20491](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20491v2)
- **[M] Prompt-A-Video (ICCV 2025; arXiv Dec 2024, older).** Reward-guided prompt evolution + preference-aligned LLM: average VBench gains of about 0.201/0.202 for Open-Sora 1.2 and 0.067 for CogVideoX (scale as reported by the summary). An unverified secondary summary gives CogVideoX VBench quality 80.98 → 83.28. — [arXiv 2412.15156](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.15156); [Lacuna summary](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/prompt-a-video-prompt-your-video-diffusion-model-via-preference-aligned-llm/wrk_aa2e1707817ec95ef5d42643720790f6)
- **[M, critique]** A review of Prompt-A-Video notes the gains are partly circular (prompts selected to maximise the metric used in training), evaluated on a self-curated 100-prompt VBench subset "with no seeds, repeat runs, or significance tests"; Open-Sora gains of AQ +0.036 and IQ +0.028 are called small. — [pith.science](https://pith.science/paper/2412.15156)
- **[M] RAPO (CVPR 2025; tested on older models LaVie / Latte).** VBench "multiple objects" rose 37.71% → 64.86% on LaVie and 29.55% → 52.78% on Latte. Ablation "final sum score": LaVie 248, **LaVie + GPT-4 rewrite 246**, LaVie + Open-Sora rewriter 251, LaVie + RAPO 256. A second block (benchmark label unclear in the extract, probably T2V-CompBench columns): baseline 0.620 / 0.232 / 0.483 / 0.760; GPT-4 rewrite 0.561 / 0.218 / 0.428 / 0.620; RAPO 0.692 / 0.267 / 0.635 / 0.839. — [arXiv 2504.11739](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11739v1)
- **[M] RAPO++.** VBench total: LaVie baseline 80.89%, generic "Prompt Refiner" 79.75%, RAPO++ 82.65% (Latte: 80.75%). — [arXiv 2510.20206](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.20206)
- **[M] VBench-2.0** applied a modified CogVideoX prompt refiner to all models and reports that, "except for Sora, Prompt Refiner consistently result in higher-quality videos with better alignment to the input text" (authors suggest Sora has an embedded refiner). — [arXiv 2503.21755](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21755v2)
- **[M, methodological]** A 2026 distillation paper notes that "almost all the baselines, including Wan2.1 base models, also enhance VBench prompts, making them longer and more descriptive" — i.e. published VBench numbers are usually *with* prompt enhancement, and prompt length confounds comparisons. — [arXiv 2601.09881](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.09881)
- **[M] WanPE (arXiv Sept 2026).** Prompt enhancer for Wan3.0's generator; ~11K blind pairwise human assessments, Bradley-Terry scoring. Human preference over **raw user prompts** rises by about 10.7-18.8 points on 5-15 s videos and by about 50.9 points on 30 s videos; gains grow with duration; enhanced prompts are reported to transfer to other generators. — [arXiv 2609.30221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30221); [project page](https://wan-pe.github.io/)
- **[M] VQQA (arXiv Mar 2026).** Agent loop (generate questions about the video → answer them → refine the prompt): +11.57% absolute on T2V-CompBench and +8.43% on VBench2 over vanilla generation, for T2V and I2V. Gemini-3-Pro variant: T2V-CompBench average 53.46% (+4.76 over the strongest baseline, VQAScore), VBench2 total 50.41% (+3.46 over VQAScore). — [arXiv 2603.12310](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.12310); [project page](https://yiwen-song.github.io/vqqa/)
- **[M] PRIS ("Rethinking Prompt Design for Inference-time Scaling", arXiv Dec 2025).** Claim: scaling seeds or sampling steps with a fixed prompt "quickly plateaus"; revising the prompt from what was generated gives "15% improvement on VBench 2.0". Venue status conflicting (OpenReview shows an ICLR 2026 withdrawn submission; Scholar lists CVPR 2026). — [arXiv 2512.03534](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.03534); [project page](https://subin-kim-cv.github.io/PRIS/); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=AsWjldujdO)
- **[M] CAPE-T2V (arXiv Aug 2026).** Names the cause: a "PE-Caption gap" between the detailed captions a T2V model is trained on and the rewritten user prompts it sees at inference. Training the enhancer on captioner targets (and fine-tuning the DiT on enhancer-rewritten captions) raises aggregate StoryEval, VBench-2.0 and T2V-CompBench scores on Wan2.2 and LTX-2.3; deltas not retrieved. — [arXiv 2608.03046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03046)
- **[M] VARI (arXiv Oct 2026).** Abstract: "meaning-preserving changes in prompt wording can substantially alter semantic realization and overall quality" (tested on HunyuanVideo-1.5 and LTX-2.3); numbers not retrieved. — [arXiv 2610.18189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18189)
- **[V] Wan 2.1.** Official repo: "Extending the prompts can effectively enrich the details in the generated videos, further enhancing the video quality. Therefore, we recommend enabling prompt extension." No with/without ablation published. — [Wan2.1 GitHub](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.1)
- **[V] Veo.** Google docs: the LLM prompt rewriter adds video description, camera motions, transcription and sound effects, and "using the prompt rewriter usually results in higher-quality generated videos"; on Vertex the rewritten prompt is returned only if the original prompt is fewer than 30 words. — [Google Cloud docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/video/turn-the-prompt-rewriter-off)
- **[V] MiniMax Hailuo API.** `prompt_optimizer` defaults to true; "Set to false for more precise control." — [MiniMax T2V API docs](https://platform.minimax.io/docs/api-reference/video-generation-t2v)
- **[V/A] H3 on fal** has a Prompt Expansion switch; "if disabled, the prompt must be compatible with the model". — [fal H3 prompting guide](https://fal.ai/learn/devs/minimax-h3-prompting-guide)
- **[A] Rewriters also do damage (H3).** One tester reports the H3 prompt expander shrank a "slow dolly-in" to a small push ("the vase grows only slightly over six seconds") and split a three-move camera instruction into separate shots with hard cuts at 1.58 s and 3.67 s; rewriting with explicit start framing, end framing and move size gave the intended result. — [Fuser](https://fuser.studio/articles/minimax-h3-prompt-guide)
- **[Project fact]** The user's self-hosted H3 pipeline has no prompt enhancement step ("提示词增强: 无") and its prompts use the lightx2v structured format the turbo LoRA was trained on (`integrated_multimodal_description` / `overall_soundscape` / `non_diegetic_music`; R2VA adds `subject_definitions` / `summary` / `retention_analysis` / `detailed_description`). — local file `/home/user/open-higgsfield-server/docs/handoff/HANDOFF.md`

### Inferences
- The consistent reading across VPO, RAPO, CAPE-T2V and the vendor rewriters: the lever is *distribution match* (prompt looks like a training caption, in the model's own schema), not "more words". For the user's H3 setup, where there is no built-in expander and the LoRA was trained on one structured schema, the compiler that writes that schema is doing the job hosted services do with their rewriter; getting the schema right is likely worth more than wordsmithing inside it.
- Gains are largest (a) from a bare one-line prompt to a dense one, (b) on longer clips, (c) on compositional items (multiple objects). Once a prompt is already dense and in-schema, the measured papers give no evidence that further polishing adds much; no paper retrieved measures "good dense prompt vs. better dense prompt".
- Closed-loop revision (VQQA, PRIS) beating best-of-N seed selection by about 3.5-4.8 points suggests the practically useful prompt work is *reactive* (fix what the take got wrong), not up-front elaboration.

### Gaps
- No public with/without ablation of prompt extension for Wan 2.x, HunyuanVideo's rewrite model, CogVideoX upsampling, Sora's or Veo's rewriter; no measured study at all on Seedance or Hailuo/H3 rewriting.
- VPO Table 1, WanPE per-duration tables, CAPE-T2V and agentic-I2V ([arXiv 2608.12290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12290), 100 VBench++ I2V pairs) deltas were not retrievable.
- Sora's recaptioning/upsampling: nothing retrieved beyond the VBench-2.0 remark.

## 2. What the official prompt guides say (Seedance, Hailuo/H3, Kling, Veo 3.x, Runway Gen-4, Sora 2)

### Takeaway
Every vendor converges on the same shape — subject + action, scene, camera, style/light, (sound) — written as concrete visible description, one main action and one camera move per shot, shots enumerated in order, references given explicit roles; and on the same "does not work" list: negatives/exclusions, redundancy, conflicting instructions, stacked actions or camera moves, vague quality words. Guides differ on timestamps (Seedance 2.0 ignores them, 2.5 and H3 accept them).

### Cited Findings
**Seedance (ByteDance / Volcengine / BytePlus)**
- **[V] Basic formula** (Ark getting-started): "提示词 = 主体 + 运动， 背景 + 运动，镜头 + 运动", in concise, accurate natural language. — [火山方舟 Seedance 入门教程](https://ark.volcengine.com/region:cn-beijing/docs/82379/2298881?lang=zh)
- **[V] Seedance 1.0 guide:** one prompt may contain several cuts ("支持在同一prompt里包含多个切镜，这些切镜会根据提示词的内容，保持主体/风格/场景的延续性"); join them with "镜头切换"; after a cut, add the features of any new person or scene. — [Seedance 1.0 系列提示词指南](https://docs.volcengine.com/docs/ark/seedance-1-0-pro-pro-fast?lang=zh)

### Inferences
- What holds across all six vendors: enumerate shots in order; one action + one camera move per shot; concrete nouns and verbs over quality adjectives; positive statement instead of exclusion; explicit reference roles. These are the cross-model rules a prompt compiler can enforce mechanically.
- The Seedance 2.0 vs 2.5 timestamp statement is a concrete case where the same written device (time-stamped beats) is honoured by one model version and ignored by the previous one — prompt structure has to be model-version-specific, which argues for a compiler per model rather than a writer's style.
- Vendor guides tell users to iterate ("start simple, add detail", "generate several and choose"), i.e. vendors themselves frame the prompt as one input in a generate-and-select loop, not as a deterministic control.

### Gaps
- The full text of the official Seedance 1.5 Pro guide (dialogue quoting, audio prompting), the Sora 2 cookbook, the Veo 3.1 guide's negative/timestamp sections, and MiniMax's own H3 prompt guide could not be read; H3 syntax above is second-hand.
- No official statement found on how many beats/shots H3 or Seedance 2.0 reliably executes per clip.

## 3. Image-to-video: what the first frame / reference fixes and what the text should contain

### Takeaway
Vendors state plainly that in I2V the image carries subject, setting, composition and look, and the text should be spent almost entirely on motion and camera; restating the image is wasted or harmful. Research agrees and goes further: I2V models over-rely on the conditioning frame to the point of suppressing motion, so the text's hard job is to get motion at all. No study was found that apportions variance between image and text.

### Cited Findings
- **[V] Runway:** "effective image to video prompts focus almost exclusively on motion"; the image is required and carries the visual information, so the text "should be almost entirely focused on describing the desired motion" (subject action, environmental motion, camera motion, motion style and timing). — [Runway I2V Prompting Guide](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/48324313115155-Image-to-Video-Prompting-Guide); [Creating with Gen-4 Video](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/37327109429011-Creating-with-Gen-4-Video)
- **[V] Kling:** do not restate the scene — the source image already establishes subject, setting and composition, and repeating it "wastes the one slot that's actually different for video: the motion itself". — [Kling I2V prompts](https://kling.ai/topics/image-to-video-prompts)
- **[V] Volcengine (Seedance):** "图生视频时请尽量上传高清高质量的图片，上传图片的质量对图生视频影响较大"; if you have a definite picture in mind, make that image with an image model first, then do I2V. — [火山方舟 Seedance 入门教程](https://ark.volcengine.com/region:cn-beijing/docs/82379/2298881?lang=zh)
- **[A]** A Kling guide adds that describing what is already in the image "can cause the model to deviate from the reference"; an H3 guide likewise says not to redescribe the start frame and to spend the prompt on motion. — [videoai.me](https://videoai.me/blog/kling-ai-image-to-video-prompts); [Fuser](https://fuser.studio/articles/minimax-h3-prompt-guide)
- **[A] H3 references:** one tester found that assigning a job to each reference image (as published guidance recommends) "made no measurable difference", and likewise for up to nine images blended into one shot. — [PixelDojo](https://pixeldojo.ai/guides/minimax-h3-prompting-guide)
- **[M] Conditional image leakage (NeurIPS 2024, older).** I2V diffusion models lean too hard on the conditioning image at large timesteps, giving under-motion; fixes are inference-time (start from an earlier timestep) or training-time (noise on the conditioning image). — [arXiv 2406.15735](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.15735)
- **[M] FlashI2V (arXiv Sept 2025)** says existing I2V paradigms cannot avoid this leakage; reports higher dynamic degree on Wan2.1-I2V-14B. — [arXiv 2509.25187](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25187)
- **[M] Adaptive Low-Pass Guidance (CVPR 2026).** Cause: over-conditioning on the high-frequency detail of the reference image in early denoising steps; fix raises VBench dynamic degree by about 33% (another version says 36%) with per-frame quality largely unchanged. — [arXiv 2506.08456](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08456)
- **[M] "Rebalancing Reference Frame Dominance" (arXiv May 2026)** identifies reference-frame dominance in self-attention as a mechanism of motion suppression; weakening it improves motion without degrading visual quality. — [arXiv 2605.19398](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.19398)
- **[M] MotiF ("Making Text Count in Image Animation", Dec 2024, older)** exists precisely because text is under-weighted in I2V; it notes VBench-I2V (355 images) uses captions and focuses on camera motion, and that flow-based motion metrics favour camera/background over object motion. — [arXiv 2412.16153](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.16153)
- **[M, vendor benchmark] Seedance 2.0 report:** I2V scores for motion quality, video prompt following and image preservation of 3.31-3.46 (apparently a 1-5 scale); the model's weakest result is video extension, task-following 1.93 vs 2.78 for Veo 3.1 on a 1-3 scale. — [arXiv 2604.14148](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.14148)

### Inferences
- In I2V/first-frame mode the look (composition, lighting, identity, style) is decided before the video prompt is written; the per-shot prompt then controls a narrower thing — what moves, how the camera moves, and (for H3/Seedance) sound. For a pipeline that generates hero frames first, the *image* prompt and frame review are where look quality is won or lost.
- Because the reference frame suppresses motion, the I2V text that matters is specific motion with magnitude (start state, end state, move size) — consistent with the Fuser dolly-in anecdote and Seedance's "motion" formula.
- Reference-to-video is intermediate: the references fix identity, but composition is free, so the text must carry framing as well as motion. The PixelDojo finding (role assignment made no measurable difference) is one tester and conflicts with every guide; treat as unresolved.

### Gaps
- No study found that quantifies the share of outcome variance due to the first frame vs. the text in I2V; UI2V-Bench ([arXiv 2509.24427](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.24427)) and Step-Video-TI2V-Eval ([arXiv 2503.11251](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2503.11251), 298 pairs) measure instruction adherence in I2V but their numbers were not retrieved.
- No vendor statement found for H3 or Seedance that says in so many words "do not describe the image".

## 4. Seed variance vs. prompt variance; take ratios

### Takeaway
For a fixed prompt, results vary enough across seeds that best-of-N selection alone moves benchmark totals by several points and practitioners plan about 3-5 generations per usable shot (6-10 for hands, lip-sync, walking, multi-character). Seed search with a fixed prompt plateaus; stable failures recur on every seed and only a prompt (or input) change removes them. No study directly decomposes human-rated quality variance into seed vs. prompt.

### Cited Findings
- **[M] Video-T1 (ICCV 2025).** Treats test-time scaling as search over initial noise (best-of-N with a verifier); VBench rises consistently with the number of noise candidates across six models. Headline: Pyramid-Flow (FLUX) total 81.61 → 86.51 with test-time scaling (another source says "5.86"; the two do not match — check the table). A 2B model with TTS approaches or exceeds a 13B model sampled once. — [arXiv 2503.18942](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18942)
- **[M] PRIS:** with a fixed prompt, scaling seeds or steps "quickly hits a performance ceiling, with outputs continuing to miss parts of the prompt even as compute grows". — [PRIS project page](https://subin-kim-cv.github.io/PRIS/)
- **[M] VQQA** prompt refinement beats VQAScore-based selection by +4.76 (T2V-CompBench) and +3.46 (VBench2) points. — [arXiv 2603.12310](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.12310)
- **[M] Paired diagnostic study of noise initialisation (arXiv Mar 2026):** on 100 prompts with VBench (automatic metrics), "prompt-level variance dominates the effect size" — differences between prompts are larger than what a noise-initialisation method changes; it also notes "different initial Gaussian noises can yield large semantic and motion variations under the same prompt". — [arXiv 2603.06672](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.06672)
- **[M] Anchored Video Generation (arXiv Dec 2025):** seed-induced diversity decomposes into scene-level (which "dominates the total variance") and motion-level components. — [arXiv 2512.16371](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.16371)
- **[A] Take ratios.** One documented 3-minute animated episode on Seedance 2.0: 164 video generations, 41 in the final cut (about 25%; planning figure about 3 per usable shot), and 17 of the 41 final shots were composites of the best seconds of several generations. Other planning figures: 3-5 generations per usable shot on mixed work, 6-10 on hands, lip-sync, walking and multi-subject scenes; a review cited by Poppify puts Kling 3.0 at 1-in-4 and Veo 3.1 at 1-in-5. All vendor/blog content. — [invideo](https://invideo.io/faq/how-many-ai-video-generations-do-you-need-per-usable/); [Poppify](https://poppify.ai/research/real-cost-ai-video-generation); [StoryLab](https://storylab.ai/how-much-does-ai-filmmaking-cost/)
- **[A] Chinese reports on Seedance 2.0 "抽卡":** media repeat "可用率从行业常见的约20%提升至90%以上"; one hands-on gives 75%+ usable (pre-launch, source unclear); one reviewer's personal estimate is 80%+ against about 70% waste before; a Zhihu comparison says Seedance 2.0 fast needs "一到两次" pulls on photoreal cases. No sample sizes. — [虎嗅](https://www.huxiu.com/article/4833593.html); [什么值得买](https://post.smzdm.com/p/a3m2mz47/); [网易](https://www.163.com/dy/article/KLB75A4J0556703U.html); [知乎](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2071534147034780065)
- **[A] Stable vs. random failures:** in a six-model adherence test (two prompts, twenty checkable elements, 2026-07-30), three repeat runs left the model ordering unchanged, and Kling 3 Turbo painted a blue-grey wall instead of the requested white in all three runs — "a stable preference rather than sampling noise"; the author says three runs are "not enough to make them a benchmark". — [aireiter](https://aireiter.com/blog/ai-video-generator-prompt-adherence-tested)
- **[V/A]** LTX's seed guide suggests 4-8 random seeds to see the range a prompt can produce. — [LTX on X](https://x.com/ltx_io/article/2056473254865317971)

### Inferences
- Two kinds of failure need two different levers: *random* faults (a hand, a flicker, a drifted face on one take) are cheapest to fix with another seed; *systematic* misses (same element dropped on every take) do not go away with more seeds and need a prompt or input change. This is the mechanism behind "seed scaling plateaus".
- Vendor/blog take ratios (25-33% usable for general shots) imply that even with competent prompts most generations are discarded; prompt work can raise the ceiling but the evidence does not show it removes the need for multiple takes. The 17-of-41 composite figure shows editing (using the good seconds) is also a lever on the ratio.
- The claimed jump from about 20% to 75-90% usable for Seedance 2.0 is attributed to the model generation, not to prompt writing — i.e. model > everything else, which is outside the fixed-model question but bounds how much prompt work can matter.

### Gaps
- No variance-component analysis (seed vs. prompt, human-rated) was found; a GitHub project with 50 prompts × 16 seeds scored by VideoReward calls its own results preliminary ([seed-experience-search](https://github.com/Charlotte06h/seed-experience-search)).
- No evidence found, measured or anecdotal with numbers, that a better-written prompt lowers the take ratio for a given shot on Seedance or H3.
- An unattributed user report surfaced in search (about 10% of H3 takes get diegetic music and about 50% get background sound despite the prompt asking for none); the source page could not be identified, so it is not cited.

## 5. Known prompt-following limits (beats, subjects, camera, text, negation, ordering)

### Takeaway
Benchmarks consistently show the weak spots are things that change over time inside one clip: attribute/state changes, ordered multi-event sequences, counts above three or so, and (in older models) rendered text. Multi-event completion stayed below 50% for every model in the 2025 benchmarks and was 45.9% for Wan2.2 in a 2026 paper. Simple single-action scenes are followed almost equally well by all current models; models separate only on sequenced prompts.

### Cited Findings
- **[M] VBench-2.0 (Mar 2025).** Models "still struggle with generating complex plots, handling simple dynamic changes in objects"; "most models perform poorly in capturing Dynamic Spatial Relationships and Dynamic Attributes"; Kling 1.6 was best on camera-related dimensions, CogVideoX-1.5 on Complex Plot. — [arXiv 2503.21755](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21755v2)
- **[M] VBench-2.0 totals now:** Stanford AI Index 2026 reports Veo 3 leading at 66.7%, Vidu Q1 at 62.7%; a mirror lists Veo3 Fast 0.618 and Wan 2.2-A14B 0.61. Even the leader is far from saturated. — [AI Index 2026 ch. 2](https://hai.stanford.edu/assets/files/ai_index_report_2026_chapter_2_technical.pdf); [SOTA2 mirror](https://www.sota2.com/research/sota/video-generation-on-vbench-2-0-overall)
- **[M] T2V-CompBench (CVPR 2025; 23 models).** Dynamic attribute binding is the hardest category, "even the best models achieving scores below 0.07" (T2V-Turbo-V2: 0.0127); numeracy: fine "when the quantities are fewer than three", failing for larger counts; object interactions, action binding and consistent attribute binding are easier. — [arXiv 2407.14505](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.14505); [project page](https://t2v-compbench-2025.github.io/); [summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/t2v-compbench)
- **[M] StoryEval (CVPR 2025).** 423 prompts of 2-4 consecutive events; none of 11 models exceeded an average story-completion rate of 50%. A 2026 paper reports Wan2.2 (original DiT) at 45.9% event completion overall. — [StoryEval GitHub](https://github.com/ypwang61/StoryEval); [arXiv 2608.03046](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.03046)
- **[M] SeqBench (Oct 2025).** Adds ordering and consequence; leader Kling 2.0 scores 0.266 on single-subject multi-action and 0.366 on multi-subject multi-action prompts; models "fail to maintain consistent object states across multi-action sequences" and struggle with timing and ordering. — [arXiv 2510.13042](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13042)
- **[M] Counting (Apr 2025):** accuracy below 10% when asked for nine objects; good for one. — [arXiv 2504.04051](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04051)
- **[M] Text rendering, T2VTextBench (May 2025, older models):** best average 0.37 (Sora); Dreamina and Kling below 0.12; Hailuo 0.5 on cinematic prompts, 0.25 on mathematical text. — [arXiv 2505.04946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04946)
- **[A] Text in 2026 models:** in a July 2026 test all six models rendered "OPEN 7AM" without a wrong character; an H3 tester found that spelling out the exact string changes *which* words appear, not whether text appears (the merely described version invented "CHAPTER I"), and calls legible text H3's biggest practical difference. — [aireiter](https://aireiter.com/blog/ai-video-generator-prompt-adherence-tested); [PixelDojo](https://pixeldojo.ai/guides/minimax-h3-prompting-guide)
- **[M, vendor benchmark] Camera:** Seedance 2.0 report, "Advanced Camera Movement" prompt-following row: Seedance 2.0 3.46, Kling 3.0 2.85, Kling 2.6 2.69, Sora 2 Pro 2.50, Veo 3.1 2.40 (scale not stated in the extract; other rows are out of 5). — [arXiv 2604.14148](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.14148)
- **[A] Camera on H3:** chained camera moves were split into cuts; a slow dolly-in was under-executed until start/end framing and move size were written. A plain prompt produced an unrequested cut at 6.54 s, a structured prompt cut on time; a bare prompt made the model "write its own film: two setups and a cut". Camera and reference control are reported more reliable than dialogue and audio; a 220-upvote Reddit thread is about gibberish dialogue. — [Fuser](https://fuser.studio/articles/minimax-h3-prompt-guide); [OmniaKey](https://omniakey.com/blog/minimax-h3-prompt-guide); [aireiter H3 review](https://aireiter.com/blog/minimax-h3-prompt-review)
- **[A]** Across six 2026 models, "on straightforward scenes accuracy was near-identical across five of six models"; ranking separates only on sequenced prompts. — [aireiter](https://aireiter.com/blog/ai-video-generator-prompt-adherence-tested)
- **[M/V] Negation.** Benchmarks include it (LOVE has a negation task, e.g. "a classroom without people"; a 2026 method paper NEGATE targets negation in T2V diffusion), and vision-language encoders are shown not to understand negation (NegBench, CVPR 2025, retrieval not generation). Runway states its model interprets what should happen, "not what should be avoided". — [LOVE, arXiv 2505.12098](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12098v1); [NegBench](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Alhamoud_Vision-Language_Models_Do_Not_Understand_Negation_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf); [NEGATE leaderboard mirror](https://www.sota2.com/research/sota/negation-aware-video-generation-benchmark); [Runway](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/39789879462419-Gen-4-Video-Prompting-Guide)

### Inferences
- The limits are structural, not wording problems: no phrasing was shown to make a model execute four ordered events or a mid-clip attribute change reliably. The effective prompt-side response is to *ask for less per generation* (one action, one camera move, split the rest into separate shots), which is a shot-list decision more than a prompt-writing one.
- Text rendering has improved sharply between the 2025 benchmark models and mid-2026 models, so old "models can't write text" numbers should be flagged as dated; the project's choice to add titles in the assembler remains exact by construction.
- "A ban is drawn as the thing it bans" is supported indirectly (encoders ignore negation; vendors tell users to state positives) but no retrieved source gives a failure rate for negated prompts in video generation.

### Gaps
- VBench-2.0 per-dimension numbers (Dynamic Attribute, Motion Order Understanding, Complex Plot, Camera Motion) could not be retrieved with labels; VideoPhy / VideoPhy-2 numbers were not found; no negation failure rate for video models; no public benchmark numbers for H3 on any of these.
- No measured figure for how many timestamped beats one H3 or Seedance clip executes reliably.

## 6. Do distilled / few-step / no-CFG models follow prompts worse?

### Takeaway
No direct measurement was found that isolates prompt adherence of a few-step student against its full-step teacher, and none at all for H3 with the lightx2v 8-step LoRA. What the literature does report is that few-step distillation tends to cost motion magnitude, high-frequency detail, diversity and physical consistency, while distillers claim parity "in most scenarios"; without CFG there is also no negative-prompt channel.

### Cited Findings
- **[M]** Transition Matching Distillation says it "can improve prompt adherence at comparable cost" relative to a DMD2-v distilled baseline (student vs. student, not vs. teacher). — [arXiv 2601.09881](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.09881)
- **[M, attribution within the cited set uncertain]** 2026 few-step papers report: a 4-step Wan method reaching 0.556 mean on VBench 2.0 as state of the art for 4 steps; pruning after distillation causing "a precipitous degradation in temporal dynamics, most notably Dynamic Degree"; "aggressive step reduction inevitably suppresses high-frequency details"; few-step students lagging the teacher in physical consistency. The search summary did not say which of these papers each sentence came from. — [arXiv 2609.39132](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39132); [arXiv 2607.06631](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06631v1); [arXiv 2606.18478](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18478)
- **[M]** One 2026 paper found a 4-step distilled Wan2.2 dropped from 0.685 to 0.605 on a video-reasoning benchmark because the first denoising step collapses too aggressively (a reasoning measure, not prompt adherence). — [arXiv 2603.16870](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.16870)
- **[V] lightx2v Wan2.2-Lightning:** the distilled model "delivers visuals on par with the base model in most scenarios, sometimes even better", runs without CFG, and its inference examples recommend prompt extension. — [lightx2v/Wan2.2-Lightning](https://huggingface.co/lightx2v/Wan2.2-Lightning)
- **[A]** Community notes on Wan 2.2 speed LoRAs: one LoRA version is "reportedly much better with prompt adherence" than others (so adherence varies by distillation release); CFG above 1 roughly doubles per-step cost, so users apply CFG only on the first step. — [wanx-troopers LoRA notes](https://wanx-troopers.github.io/loras/part-01.html)
- **[V/A] H3 turbo:** the lightx2v 8-step 768p LoRA is recommended at 8 steps, video shift 6, audio shift 3, Euler/Simple, and is said to improve quality over the earlier 4-step release; the base model runs at about 20 steps; MiniMax's model card lists no distilled checkpoint (the turbo LoRAs come from LightX2V/ModelTC). No side-by-side quality or adherence comparison with the 20-step base was found. — [lightx2v/Minimax-h3-Turbo discussion](https://huggingface.co/lightx2v/Minimax-h3-Turbo/discussions/48); [ModelTC/Minimax-H3-Turbo](https://github.com/ModelTC/Minimax-H3-Turbo); [minimax3.com](https://minimax3.com/tools/minimax-h3-turbo)
- **[Project fact]** The user's pipeline: turbo 8 steps, euler/simple, shift 6.0/3.0, BasicGuider (CFG effectively 1) for both turbo and the earlier 20-step base. — local file `/home/user/open-higgsfield-server/docs/handoff/HANDOFF.md`

### Inferences
- With CFG at 1 there is no unconditional branch being pushed away from, so text conditioning is not amplified at sampling time; any prompt-following strength has to be baked in by distillation at a fixed guidance scale. This plausibly makes prompt *format* (matching what the LoRA was trained on) matter more, and makes exclusions impossible to enforce — consistent with the project's "no negative prompt" rule. This is reasoning from mechanism, not a measured result.
- The reported costs of few-step sampling (less motion, less detail, less seed diversity — the last implied by the title "Restoring Diversity and Fidelity in Few-Step Video Generation") suggest that on a turbo model, re-rolling the seed buys less variety than on the full model, which would shift some weight back to prompt/input changes. Untested for H3.
- Since the project can run both 8-step turbo and 20-step base on the same prompt, an in-house A/B on adherence is the only way to answer this for H3.

### Gaps
- No teacher-vs-student prompt-adherence table (VBench semantic, T2V-CompBench, or human) retrieved for any few-step video model; nothing for H3 turbo or for Seedance's distilled "fast" variants (the Seedance 1.0 paper's "10×" speedup is known only from a secondary summary: [arXiv 2506.09113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09113)).
- No source found that tests negative-prompt behaviour at CFG 1 for video models.

## 7. Overall: with the model fixed, is the per-shot prompt the main lever or a secondary one?

### Takeaway
On the evidence found, the per-shot prompt is a real but bounded lever: it is decisive for *what happens* (action, camera, order, sound) and for avoiding systematic misses, and it must be in the model's trained format; but the look is set by the first frame/references, random faults are fixed by extra takes, and what a single generation can execute is capped by the model. The measured size of pure prompt improvement on short clips (about +4 to +19 points depending on metric and baseline) is of the same order as best-of-N seed selection, and the two are complementary.

### Cited Findings
- **[M]** Prompt enhancement vs. raw prompt on 5-15 s clips: +10.7 to +18.8 human-preference points; much more (+50.9) at 30 s. — [arXiv 2609.30221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30221)
- **[M]** Best-of-N seed search alone: about +4.9 VBench points on one model. — [arXiv 2503.18942](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18942)
- **[M]** Prompt revision in a loop beats selection alone by +3.46 to +4.76 points and beats vanilla by +8.43 to +11.57. — [arXiv 2603.12310](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.12310)
- **[M]** Naive rewriting can be worse than no rewriting (LaVie 248 vs. GPT-4 rewrite 246; baseline 80.89% vs. generic refiner 79.75%). — [arXiv 2504.11739](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11739v1); [arXiv 2510.20206](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.20206)
- **[V]** In I2V the image carries subject, setting and composition; text is for motion. — [Runway](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/37327109429011-Creating-with-Gen-4-Video); [Kling](https://kling.ai/topics/image-to-video-prompts)
- **[M]** Multi-event prompts complete under 50% of the time regardless of wording. — [StoryEval](https://github.com/ypwang61/StoryEval)
- **[A]** Practitioners still plan 3-5 generations per usable shot. — [Poppify](https://poppify.ai/research/real-cost-ai-video-generation)

### Inferences
- A defensible ordering of levers for a fixed model in an I2V / reference-driven pipeline: (1) the inputs that fix the look — hero frame and references; (2) what is asked of one generation — one action, one camera move, shot splitting (a shot-list decision); (3) the prompt being in the trained schema and stating motion concretely with magnitude; (4) number of takes plus selection; (5) fine wording inside an already well-formed prompt — smallest, and unmeasured.
- For an LLM agent this implies the agent's value is less in "writing beautifully" and more in (a) compiling to the exact schema, (b) enforcing the per-shot limits before spending, and (c) reading a failed take and revising the prompt or inputs — the closed-loop role that the measured papers show beating both fixed prompts and seed search. Note the project's stated constraint that the agent sees stills, never motion or sound, which limits (c) to what a frame shows.
- All of this is cross-model evidence; none of it was measured on H3 or Seedance by an independent party.

### Gaps
- No controlled experiment found, for any current commercial model, that varies prompt quality, first frame, and seed in one design and reports their relative contributions.
- No independent measurement of prompt sensitivity on MiniMax H3 (base or turbo) or on Seedance 2.0/2.5; vendor numbers for Seedance come from ByteDance's own SeedVideoBench 2.0.
