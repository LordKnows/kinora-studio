## 1. What workflows do the praised films describe, and where do the makers say the time and the quality come from?

### Takeaway
Across Hollywood-adjacent shorts (ANCESTRA, Air Head, Dear Upstairs Neighbors), the one all-AI feature with published numbers (Hell Grind) and Chinese AI 漫剧 shops, the pattern is the same: images first, then animation, then a very large discard pile, then conventional editing and finishing. Makers place the time in generation-and-selection and the quality in what was decided before generation (story, look, shot design) and after it (the cut and the finish); critics say the failure of the weakest films is story and believable people, not picture quality.

For a scene of organic holes closing, the team gave Veo reference videos of the motion and prompted it to match that motion across shots. The film is a hybrid: live-action performance, compositing and editing are part of it. **[maker/vendor]** — [Google blog](https://blog.google/technology/google-deepmind/ancestra-behind-the-scenes/)

### Inferences
- **Ranked list of what matters most for a cinematic result with the model fixed** (my synthesis; the evidence grade is in brackets).
  1. **A story and a point of view held by someone before generation starts** [strong, convergent]. The most expensive all-AI film with published numbers was reviewed as realistic-looking and nonsensical; the Chinese hit rate under 0.5% and "剧本理解/审美 scarce" say the same from the market side. Picture quality did not rescue weak story anywhere in the sources.
  2. **Images before video: lock look, cast, locations and each shot's first frame at the cheap image stage** [strong, convergent]. ANCESTRA, the UAE winner, Ulysses, Asteria and the Chinese line all generate and approve stills first; the Chinese shops call the stills "种子".
  3. **Budget for selection and build a way to do it** [strong on the numbers, weak on method]. Reported ratios run from about 4:1 to 300:1 (see section 3). Dailies, a shared library of generations and an editor's eye are what the best teams describe.
  4. **Consistency carried by assets, not by prompts** [strong]. Reference kits re-attached to every shot at the low end; per-production custom models at the high end.
  5. **Shot design that fits what the model holds: short, one idea, one camera move, varied sizes and angles, action the camera can see** [moderate; mostly tutorials].
  6. **The cut and the finish: conventional editing, a single grade and grain over everything, clean-up, upscale** [moderate; Air Head and Asteria first-hand, the rest vendor].
  7. **Believable people: performance, dialogue, lip-sync** [strong that it is the weak point; weak on fixes]. Critics name it as the remaining gap, and Chinese producers route around it with live actors.
  8. **Sound** [weak evidence in what I could reach]. Festivals give it its own awards and a composer won AIFF 2025, but I found no first-hand statement that quantifies its contribution.
- Where the time goes and where the quality comes from are different stages. Time is in generation and selection (half of a Chinese team's heads; 16,181 generations for 25 minutes). Quality is credited to decisions on either side of that stage.
- The teams with the best reputations reduce randomness with control inputs (video-to-video, reference motion, plates, animatics, custom models) rather than with better prose in the prompt.

### Gaps
- I could not read any of the making-of pages in full, so quotes are limited to what search extracts carried. Dave Clark's No Film School interview (https://nofilmschool.com/dave-clark-interview-adobe-max), the Curious Refuge professional workflow post (https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/how-to-create-an-ai-film) and the Guardian piece on Dreams of Violets were found but not read.
- Nothing usable was found on workflows at Wonder Studios, on the Bionic Awards (the search returned nothing by that name), on Kling or 即梦 competition winners, or on Chroma Awards judging criteria. Chroma Awards Season 1 (formerly Project Odyssey, incubated by ElevenLabs) had more than 6,500 submissions per one guide — [aifilmcontests.com](https://aifilmcontests.com/topics/chroma-awards-season-2-2026) — but no rubric surfaced.
- No source breaks total hours down by stage. "Half the team is 抽卡" is the closest proxy.

## 2. How important is the image-first pipeline against text-to-video, and does the first frame determine the shot?

### Takeaway
Image-first is the default among every production in the sample that explains itself, and practitioners state the causal claim directly: the still is frame 0 and its look, identity and composition carry into the clip. The evidence that first-frame quality sets the ceiling is consistent but comes mostly from tutorials and vendor guides, not from measurements.

### Cited Findings
- ANCESTRA: Imagen/Veo candidate images were organised by mood, colour and emotion, and Veo then animated the chosen images. **[maker/vendor]** — [Google blog](https://blog.google/technology/google-deepmind/ancestra-behind-the-scenes/)
- A Chinese production-workflow essay argues for pushing uncertainty into the cheap stages: 风格定调、人物统一、姿态约束 are done up front, trial and repair happen at the image stage, and only validated base images go to video generation ("通过前置约束、自动化品控与分层生成等机制，将不确定性消化在低成本环节"). **[commentary/practitioner]** — [人人都是产品经理](https://www.woshipm.com/ai/6356310.html)
- "The better the generated images and the more consistent the details, the better the video output from Seedance will be"; as reference-image quality improved, "the overall output quality across all downstream processes followed suit" (said of GPT Image 2 feeding Seedance 2.0). **[press flash, thin sourcing]** — [KuCoin news](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/seedance-2-0-and-gpt-image-2-0-revolutionize-ai-short-drama-production)
- A start frame "becomes the exact first frame of the generated video clip", and its visual style generally sets the aesthetic of the clip; match its aspect ratio to the output. **[vendor glossary]** — [Morphic](https://morphic.com/ai-glossary/start-frame)
- One guide calls the still "the literal frame 0 of the output" and recommends at least 1024 px on the long edge, with 1536 to 2048 px as the sweet spot; another advises making the end frame by editing the start frame, not by generating a second image from scratch, so identity holds. I could not tell which of the two pages carries which statement. **[vendor guides]** — [clipspeed.ai](https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/image-to-video-ai-workflow.html); [rangy.ai](https://rangy.ai/blog/image-to-video-ai-workflow/)
- Curious Refuge on first/last frame: "By locking in where your shot starts and where it ends, you gain far more creative control over the animation in between." **[educator]** — [Curious Refuge](https://curiousrefuge.com/blog/8-ways-to-use-first-frame-last-frame-in-your-ai-videos)
- MiniMax shipped start and end frames for Hailuo 02 on 2025-08-28, so the H3 family supports both anchors. **[vendor]** — [MiniMax](https://www.minimax.io/news/minimax-hailuo-02-start-end-frames-feature-is-now-live)
- The Eian workflow (Claude Code as director, Codex as painter, Seedance as animator): Codex draws 设定图, 样帧 and each shot's 首尾帧; Seedance turns the first and last frames into a few seconds of video. **[anecdotal, first-hand]** — [X/@EianLu](https://x.com/EianLu/status/2118317430665613741)
- The cost of control, per Huxiu: a "控制悖论" in which the creator has to absorb the failures of image generation first in order to get controllable video. **[press]** — [虎嗅](https://www.huxiu.com/article/4840824.html)
- Counter-current: Seedance 2.0 reviews say the model plans 分镜 and camera moves from a story description alone, which one essay reads as collapsing the storyboard-and-assemble workflow into one multimodal pass. The same reviews advise doing concrete 分镜 first and using it as reference when strict control is needed. **[press/reviews]** — [钛媒体](https://www.tmtpost.com/7876646.html); [腾讯新闻](https://view.inews.qq.com/a/20260208A03LTP00); [人人都是产品经理](https://www.woshipm.com/ai/6342108.html)

### Inferences
- Image-first is doing three jobs at once: it fixes identity and art direction, it moves the randomness to where a retry costs cents, and it gives a reviewer something inspectable before the expensive step. An agent that sees stills but not motion gets most of its leverage here.
- Text-to-video with model-side multi-shot planning is good enough for volume content and for exploration; none of the praised films in this sample describe using it for final shots.
- "First frame determines the shot" is best stated as: the first frame sets the ceiling for look, identity and composition; motion, performance and physics are still a draw.

### Gaps
- No controlled comparison of image-to-video against text-to-video on the same shots was found.
- No maker statement specific to H3 or Seedance on how often a good first frame still yields a bad take.

## 3. What is the shooting ratio, how are takes selected, and how much is curation and editing rather than prompting?

### Takeaway
Reported ratios span two orders of magnitude, from about 4:1 on simple work to 64:1 on a feature and about 300:1 on an early Sora short; Chinese shops quote a 20% 抽卡率 as normal. Selection is described as human viewing of motion, and the makers who talk about it frame the film as found in the edit.

### Cited Findings
- Hell Grind: 16,181 generations for 253 final shots in the first 25 minutes, 64:1. **[vendor figure via press]** — [Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/news/in-pictures-higgsfield-unveils-fully-ai-generated-feature-hell-grind-in-cannes/5216871.article)
- Air Head: about 300:1 source to final (Patrick Cederberg's estimate). **[maker]** — [fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/fxfeatured/actually-using-sora/)
- ANCESTRA: sometimes 200 to 1,000 generations for one usable image. **[maker, via press]** — [IBM Think](https://www.ibm.com/think/news/how-filmmakers-are-reimagining-cinema-with-ai)
- Total Pixel Space: tens of thousands of images over more than a year. **[press]** — [AP via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/film-festival-showcases-artificial-intelligence-153246905.html)
- A Chinese team reports an internal 抽卡率 of about 20%: five generations for one usable shot. **[press]** — [搜狐](https://www.sohu.com/a/1030284284_114986)
- Another team says a standardised workflow cut the draws needed from "十几次" to "两三次"; the same report says limb collapse, face changes and broken shot logic are still common, so script review up front and retouching at the back have grown. **[press]** — [杭州日报](https://ori.hangzhou.com.cn/ornews/content/2026-10/19/content_9345947.htm)
- Huxiu on Kling 3.0: of 10 generated clips, 2 were usable. **[press]** — [虎嗅](https://www.huxiu.com/article/4840824.html)
- A Zhihu answerer reports a usable rate above 90% with Seedance 2.0; this is one person's sample. A SegmentFault article's "废片率降至10%以下" has no stated source and contradicts other reviews on clip length. **[anecdotal / unreliable]** — [知乎](https://www.zhihu.com/question/2003476733446349730); [SegmentFault](https://segmentfault.com/a/1190000047604020)
- A vendor FAQ on an unnamed production: 164 clips generated, 41 in the final cut (25%). The same page is summarised as "an average of 3 generations per usable shot", which does not match 164/41 = 4. **[vendor]** — [invideo](https://invideo.io/faq/how-many-ai-video-generations-do-you-need-per-usable/)
- Hailuo's own knowledge page says matching a complex camera trajectory across a cut often takes two or three regenerations. **[vendor]** — [hailuoai.video](https://hailuoai.video/knowledge/trajectory-matching-seamless-ai-video-cuts)
- Selection practice: the Dear Upstairs Neighbors team reviewed shots in dailies; the ANCESTRA team organised candidates by mood, colour and emotion and shared them in common tooling; in the Eian workflow the human "看动态、听声音" while the agent checks each image and clip. **[maker; anecdotal]** — [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/dear-upstairs-neighbors-hybrid-ai-short-film); [Google blog](https://blog.google/technology/google-deepmind/ancestra-behind-the-scenes/); [X/@EianLu](https://x.com/EianLu/status/2118317430665613741)
- Curation as authorship: Shy Kids wove the story from the material "documentary-style"; the Dor Brothers "assemble the best clips using traditional digital video editing tools". **[maker, via press]** — [fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/fxfeatured/actually-using-sora/); [The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/meta-google-and-other-giants-slice-up-text-to-video)
- Slop defined by the missing step: Kapwing's report says "the defining characteristic of slop is not automation itself, but the absence of editorial oversight"; a JMIR paper defines slop by the degree of human care in production. **[research/industry report]** — [NextShark on Kapwing](https://nextshark.com/videos-ai-slop-new-youtube-users); [JMIR Medical Education](https://mededu.jmir.org/2025/1/e80084)

### Inferences
- The ratio depends on the bar and on how much was fixed before the draw. A locked first frame with one simple motion sits near 3 to 5; a feature with action and recurring cast sat at 64. A planning number for an agent-run studio on H3 or Seedance is therefore a range by shot type, not a constant.
- Ratios are not comparable across sources: some count images, some clips, some include exploration. I would not average them.
- Since selection is where a 64:1 pile becomes a film, take review (motion, performance, continuity against neighbours) is a first-class stage; a system that can only judge stills needs a human or a video-capable reviewer at exactly this point.

### Gaps
- No source describes explicit take-selection criteria or a rubric; "审美" and "镜头思维" are as specific as it gets.
- No attribution study separates the contribution of prompting from curation. The claim that curation dominates is the makers' framing, supported by the ratios.

## 4. What makes AI footage read as cinematic rather than as slop?

### Takeaway
The tells named most often are independent-clip seams (backgrounds that shift between cuts), floaty slow motion and over-smooth movement, stacked or unmotivated camera moves, and people who do not convince. The remedies practitioners give are short single-idea shots, one camera move, varied angle and size, a single grade and grain across everything, and sound; nearly all of this comes from tutorials and vendor guides rather than from editors, cinematographers or juries on record.

### Cited Findings
- Tells: each cut is generated independently, so backgrounds shift between cuts in ways physics would not allow; the model's attention goes to the subject, so extras and background detail are neglected; "weirdly smooth motion with no micro-jitter". **[vendor blog]** — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ai-slop-aesthetic-12-tells)
- Slow motion: overpacked motion, mixed camera moves and unclear timing push models toward safe slow pacing; negative instructions such as "no slow motion" tend to fail. **[vendor blog]** — [Creatide](https://creatide.ai/blog/ai-video-slow-motion-why-clips-look-too-slow-and-how-to-fix-it)
- Camera: chaotic motion "is almost always a camera issue"; stacking camera instructions confuses the model; keep one move (a slow push-in, a gentle pan) or none. **[anecdotal]** — [Medium](https://medium.com/@inchristiely/why-your-ai-videos-look-wrong-and-how-to-fix-them-without-starting-over-6b6b6c48be69)
- Shot length: three to five seconds is where these models hold together; past that faces soften and backgrounds drift. **[anecdotal]** — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/xinyue_zhan_35ea4c0965196/why-your-generated-video-looks-cheap-three-fixes-in-the-first-five-minutes-3na1)
- Chinese creator testing ("2000+次实测"): "每个镜头保持 3-5 秒最符合观众注意力规律". Another guide gives about 3 seconds per 景别 change as a reference but says dwell time must follow the story and an emotional close-up can run longer. **[anecdotal]** — [优设网](https://www.uisdc.com/ai-video-mastery); [知乎专栏](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2035456872304657933)
- Angle variety (Eian's rule): across the whole film, at most two eye-level frontal shots; the rest are high, low, from behind, over the shoulder or shot through something. **[anecdotal, first-hand]** — [X/@EianLu](https://x.com/EianLu/status/2118317430665613741)
- Texture: Wong Kar-wai style handheld "最怕太干净"; keep some instability, smear and neon reflection. **[anecdotal]** — [腾讯新闻](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260709A0BINF00)
- Generic prompts make generic footage: prompts default to phrases like "cinematic drone shot" because they reliably look impressive whether or not the scene needs them; one shot should carry one idea. **[vendor blog]** — [Domer](https://domer.io/blog/script-to-shot-list-ai-cinematic-planning)
- Finish: normalise white balance and exposure per clip against scopes; one film-emulation LUT at partial opacity; a light blur (about 0.3 to 0.6 px) to soften AI sharpness, then grain (about 8 to 15% opacity); match every clip to one reference still; lock a palette with hex values at generation time. The numbers are a starting point from a vendor. **[vendor]** — [invideo](https://invideo.io/blog/ai-color-grading/); [invideo FAQ](https://invideo.io/faq/how-do-you-add-film-grain-blur-and-color-grading-to-ai/)
- Air Head's finishing pass (stabilise, upscale, colour-correct, paint out) is the first-hand version of the same point; Shy Kids said the untouched footage lacked continuity and cohesion. **[maker]** — [fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/fxfeatured/actually-using-sora/)
- Performance: TechRadar on Hell Grind, AI "still struggles with the thing that makes films memorable: believable people"; JoBlo cites jittery frame rates and weak action choreography; Seedance 2.0 reviewers note a character already on the ground standing again in the next shot and hands deforming in fights. **[press]** — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/i-watched-a-90-minute-ai-movie-made-in-just-two-weeks-and-hollywood-can-stop-worrying-for-now); [JoBlo](https://www.joblo.com/we-saw-a-full-length-ai-generated-feature/); [腾讯新闻](https://view.inews.qq.com/a/20260208A03LTP00)
- Sound: festivals now give it separate awards (Soulscape 2026 "Best Sound Design" to Giuseppe Celestino's "What Stays"; Reply AI Film Festival 2026 has a category for fully AI-generated sound and music). Seedance 2.0's native audio and lip-sync are reported as stable enough to cut post work. A Chinese guide's example of a thriller recipe: 低频环境音, 雨声, 远处车流声, 短信提示音, with a unified cold grade. **[press/festival pages; anecdotal]** — [LaBasad](https://www.labasad.com/en/?p=27143); [Reply](https://challenges.reply.com/challenges/creative/aifilmfestival/how-it-works/); [KuCoin news](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/seedance-2-0-and-gpt-image-2-0-revolutionize-ai-short-drama-production); [知乎专栏](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2035456872304657933)
- An ACM paper, "What Makes AI Slop (or Not)", is summarised as arguing the problem is less the medium than a lack of human creative oversight, alongside missing physics, spatial consistency and anatomy. I could not read it. **[research, unverified summary]** — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3799818.3812064)

### Inferences
- The slop tells are mostly seams between independently generated clips and defaults nobody overrode (slow push-in, eye-level frontal, glossy sharpness). A cinematic read comes from decisions that vary on purpose: size, angle, length, and whether the camera moves at all.
- The 3 to 5 second advice has two different justifications that happen to agree: models degrade past it, and contemporary cutting rhythm sits there. Neither is a measured quality finding for H3; the project's own notes say shot length against quality is unmeasured, and nothing found here changes that.
- "No slow motion" failing as a negative instruction matches the rule that these models draw what a prompt names; pace has to be stated as what happens and when.
- A single grade and grain pass is the cheapest way to hide model-to-model and shot-to-shot differences, and it is the one finishing step every source that mentions finishing agrees on.

### Gaps
- I found no on-record statement from a working editor, cinematographer or festival jury on shot length, coverage or grain in AI films. The Variety VIP piece on AIFF 2025 ("'Imperfect' but Important Creative Evolution", https://variety.com/vip/runway-ai-film-festival-1235993270) likely has some and could not be opened.
- No measured average shot length for any praised AI film was found.
- No first-hand maker statement quantifying sound's share of the result was found; the "sound is half the film" idea is unsupported by anything retrieved here.

## 5. Which classic film-craft rules transfer, and how are they enforced across independently generated clips?

### Takeaway
The continuity system transfers whole, but the model does not know it, so makers enforce it by restating geometry in every prompt, by chaining frames across cuts, and by re-attaching the same references to every shot. MiniMax itself publishes Hailuo guidance on shot/reverse-shot spatial logic and on matching camera trajectories across cuts.

### Cited Findings
- The 180-degree line: the camera stays on one side of the axis between two subjects; crossing it reverses left-right geography. Wikipedia notes empirical research indicating the rule is not strictly necessary in practice, so it is a convention that can be broken on purpose. **[reference]** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/180-degree_rule)
- Enforcement by prompt: state the camera side in every prompt for a scene because the model has no concept of the line; also fix camera height and lens, for example "camera at seated eye height, 85mm lens, same side of the table as the wider shot". I could not tell which of the two glossary pages carries which sentence. **[vendor glossaries]** — [ZOOOP](https://zooop.ai/glossary/editing/continuity-editing); [M Studio](https://mstudio.ai/glossary/continuity-editing)
- Hailuo "Field Notes": keep motion continuous across 4 to 6 second clips by having each new clip's prompt reference the previous shot's camera movement; pick the next shot whose motion best fits the previous clip's exit path; a clip ending on a pan followed by a static frame breaks momentum; pair moves across shots (a pan left, then a subtle tilt up); use the last frame of one shot as the first frame of the next to hold lighting and subject placement. The page admits a complex match often takes two or three regenerations. These pages read as marketing and show no data. **[vendor]** — [hailuoai.video, trajectory matching](https://hailuoai.video/knowledge/trajectory-matching-seamless-ai-video-cuts); [hailuoai.video, multi-axis camera](https://hailuoai.video/pages/knowledge/ai-video-multi-axis-camera-control)
- Hailuo also publishes "The Director's Guide to Shot-Reverse-Shot Spatial Logic"; I have the title only. **[vendor]** — [hailuoai.video](https://hailuoai.video/knowledge/director-guide-shot-reverse-shot-spatial-logic)
- Identity across cuts: each clip is a separate event and appearance drifts; upload the same character reference images for every clip in a sequence; experienced creators keep a "reference kit" per character with several angles and expressions. Seedance 2.0 does not "remember" a character, so every generation has to @ the same character image again. **[vendor guide; anecdotal]** — [Mootion](https://mootion.com/ugc/en/use-case/seedance-2-0-character-consistency-guide); [知乎](https://www.zhihu.com/question/2003476733446349730)
- Asset libraries: 角色设定, 场景风格 and 道具规范 are kept as a standardised library and called on every time. **[practitioner]** — [人人都是产品经理](https://www.woshipm.com/ai/6356310.html)
- Motion matching by reference: ANCESTRA gave Veo reference video of a motion and prompted it to match across different shots. **[maker/vendor]** — [Google blog](https://blog.google/technology/google-deepmind/ancestra-behind-the-scenes/)
- In-model multi-shot has limits: with Seedance 2.0, more than two or three shot changes in one prompt tends to fall apart, single generations top out around 15 seconds, and reviewers advise writing by the second and stitching shorter segments; Kling 3.0's multi-shot is described as reliable for cuts within a scene, not for whole sequences. **[reviews]** — [知乎](https://www.zhihu.com/question/2003476733446349730); [腾讯新闻](https://view.inews.qq.com/a/20260208A03LTP00); [Kompozy](https://kompozy.io/reviews/kling-ai-3-0)
- Story-state continuity fails too: a character on the ground in one shot stands in the next. **[review]** — [腾讯新闻](https://view.inews.qq.com/a/20260208A03LTP00)
- Before listing shots, write the scene objective, the character objective and the continuity risks. **[vendor blog]** — [Ciaro](https://ciaro.pro/blog/script-to-shot-list-ai-start-with-breakdown-not-prompts)

### Inferences
- Because the model holds no scene state, continuity has to live in data the pipeline owns: an axis and camera side per scene, a lens and height per setup, a state per character (standing, wounded, holding the prop), and the frame handed across each cut. A rule that exists only as prose in a guide will not be held.
- Three enforcement mechanisms recur and are complementary: restate (geometry in every prompt), chain (last frame to first frame, motion direction across the cut), and anchor (the same references on every shot). Match on action is the hardest of the classic cuts under this regime, since it needs the exit and entry of a movement to agree across two draws.
- Chaining frames trades cut freedom for continuity: it suits continuous action and in-scene cuts, and it works against shot-size variety if overused.

### Gaps
- No first-hand account from a praised film of how eyeline match or match on action was achieved; the guidance found is vendor or tutorial.
- The Hailuo shot/reverse-shot guide could not be read; it is the most model-specific source and worth fetching.
- No evidence on how often viewers notice a broken line in AI films specifically.

## 6. What role do makers give LLMs, and where do they help or hurt?

### Takeaway
LLMs are used for prompt development, script-to-shot-list breakdown, language editing and, in the newest creator workflows, as the "director" that writes the story, the shot list and every prompt, checks every still and clip, and codes the post. The limits makers state are that the LLM does not watch motion or hear sound, that it piles ideas into a prompt, and that it reaches for stock "cinematic" phrasing; the human keeps direction, look approval, spend and the judgement of moving picture.

### Cited Findings
- ANCESTRA used Gemini to develop prompts. **[maker/vendor]** — [Google blog](https://blog.google/technology/google-deepmind/ancestra-behind-the-scenes/)
- Dreams of Violets used Claude for language editing. **[press]** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreams_of_Violets)
- Dave Clark (Promise) recommends asking GPT to write the Midjourney prompt and describes a custom text-to-image GPT as an active collaborator while telling a story; images then go to Runway; ElevenLabs converts his own voice acting into character voices. **[maker; 2024-era interview]** — [Every](https://every.to/chain-of-thought/how-a-hollywood-director-uses-ai-to-make-movies)
- Eian's division of labour: "Claude 当导演，写故事和分镜，写所有提示词，检查每张图和每段视频，写代码做后期；Codex 画设定图、样帧和每个镜头的首尾帧；Seedance 把首尾帧变成几秒钟的视频." The creator chooses direction, confirms the look, approves spend, and "看动态、听声音". **[anecdotal, first-hand]** — [X/@EianLu](https://x.com/EianLu/status/2118317430665613741)
- Open-source skills derived from that workflow: about eight steps (想法 → 定稿剧情 → 分镜表 → 定画风和色表 → 首尾帧 → 视频提示词 with a separate agent to audit them → 生成视频); render the hardest shot first to validate the approach before the batch; each gate has an independent review and the next opens only when the last passes; one variant makes Codex the director, so the role is interchangeable. **[anecdotal/open source]** — [xdlkc/ai-video-production](https://github.com/xdlkc/ai-video-production); [AsLSX99/ai-video-workflow](https://github.com/AsLSX99/ai-video-workflow); [kowloonzh/ai-video-workflow](https://github.com/kowloonzh/ai-video-workflow)
- Prompt-writing lesson from the same workflow: rewrite abstract emotion as visible action with a checkable start and result (not "感受到温暖" but a concrete act and its outcome), because the model can execute and the reviewer can check an action. **[anecdotal, first-hand]** — [X/@EianLu](https://x.com/EianLu/status/2118317430665613741)
- Over-description: cramming several narrative ideas into one prompt gives muddled results; one shot, one idea. Generic defaults such as "cinematic drone shot" appear because they reliably look impressive, not because the scene needs them. Write the story first and test a few representative shots to learn the model's limits. **[vendor blog]** — [Domer](https://domer.io/blog/script-to-shot-list-ai-cinematic-planning)
- Runway describes AI shot-list tools as parsing a script and proposing framing, and says a human should check for what the model misses. **[vendor]** — [Runway](https://runway.com/resources/ai-shot-list)
- "Writing the Edit": with multi-shot models the prompt has to behave like a shot list, with timed cut markers inside one generation. **[commentary]** — [Micheal Lanham, Substack](https://micheallanham.substack.com/p/writing-the-edit-the-new-ai-filmmaking)
- Higgsfield says of its MCP connector that Claude "writes the prompt, calls the right Higgsfield generator, watches the result and suggests fixes"; a third-party write-up has the creator say "run the manifest", approve the cost, and Claude execute each call in order. **[vendor; anecdotal]** — [Higgsfield blog](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/claude-higgsfield-mcp-creative-studio); [Medium](https://medium.com/@Pop123/i-turned-claude-into-a-faceless-video-studio-for-higgsfield-heres-the-exact-workflow-a14a29ed83ee)
- Chinese studios have turned 抽卡 into an automated flow and plan the same for editing, per 36氪; the people they still cannot find are those with 分镜 and 编剧 skill. **[press]** — [36氪](https://www.36kr.com/p/3827470501122690); [搜狐](https://www.sohu.com/a/1030339808_122014422)

### Inferences
- The stable division in 2026 creator practice: the LLM holds the plan (story, shot list, prompts, review of stills, post code), image and video models execute, and the human holds taste, money and the judgement of motion and sound. Gate reviews by a separate agent are appearing independently in several skills, which suggests makers found that the authoring agent does not catch its own errors.
- Where LLMs hurt, by the sources' account, is in the writing habits they bring to prompts and stories: several ideas per shot, feeling words in place of action, and stock cinematic vocabulary. Each has a structural counter (one idea per shot, action with a visible result, a look defined once and not re-described).
- "Render the hardest shot first" is a cheap risk control worth copying: it tests whether the route works before the batch spends.

### Gaps
- I found no maker on record calling LLM-written stories generic; the closest is the review of Hell Grind's plot and Chinese reports of 内容同质化 — [新华网](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260429/b541e4e3478c4a43916c4777eb9b8b80/c.html) — neither of which attributes the sameness to LLM authorship.
- No comparison of ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini for film planning was found.
- The Eian post and the three repositories were not read in full; their cost, failure-rate and review-criteria details are not captured here.

### Inferences
- What the products automate is the part makers already treat as mechanical: turning a plan into calls. What reviewers fault in the output is the part no product claims to automate: whether the story holds and whether the people convince.
- The contrast inside this sample is instructive. The films with the best reception (ANCESTRA, Dear Upstairs Neighbors) had the most human control per shot and the least reliance on prompting; the film with the most automation and the largest generation count drew the weakest notes on story. This is a correlation across very different projects, not a controlled result.
- For an agent-run studio the defensible position is the one the creator skills converged on: the agent runs plan, prompts, calls and still-level review behind gates; a human approves look, spend and the moving picture; and the system's structure, not its prose guidance, carries continuity and the plan.
