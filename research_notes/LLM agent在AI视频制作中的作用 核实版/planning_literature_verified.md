# LLM agents as director / planner in multi-shot AI video: verification ledger and new findings (read from primary papers, 2026-11-13)

> **Method.** Every paper below was downloaded from arxiv.org (HTML full text, latest version unless a version is named; PDF where no HTML exists) and the tables were read directly. Nothing here comes from search snippets. "Report" = `reports/LLM agent在AI视频制作中的作用.md`; "earlier notes" = `research_notes/LLM agent在AI视频制作中的作用/llm_director_planning_literature.md`. Verdicts: CONFIRMED / CORRECTED / NOT FOUND IN PAPER / PAPER NOT REACHABLE. No paper was unreachable. Values marked "computed here" are my arithmetic on table cells, not numbers printed in the paper.

> **CORRECTIONS THAT CHANGE WHAT THE REPORT SAYS (read these first)**
>
> 1. **"No paper uses H3 or Seedance as the fixed backbone" is false.** *CineEvolve* (arXiv 2610.29590, 21 Oct 2026) runs itself and its agentic baselines on "the eight-step distilled MiniMax-H3 Turbo" (reference: LightX2V Team model card), the same stack as this project. *CineForge* (arXiv 2608.29621) runs four agent systems on Seedance 2.0. The Script paper's Table 2 has a Seedance1.5-Pro row. PersonaShot and MSAVBench evaluate Seedance 2.0 directly, OSOD compares against a product built on it, and DirectorBench evaluates ByteDance's Dreamina agent (video model not named). None of the papers I read puts raw H3 or raw Seedance 2.0 (no agent) next to an agent on that same model. Details in section 3.
> 2. **ViMax 4.107 vs 3.658 is not a video measurement.** Table 3 scores *storyboard text* with an LLM judge (GPT-5.4) on 50 NarrativeQA novels, with video synthesis disabled. The StoryboardArtist ablation "preserves story order and shot count" and removes only "agent-based visual articulation"; the sub-score that falls (VSU 4.94 → 3.10) is "Visual Specificity and Shot Usability", i.e. how concrete each shot's description is. It is evidence about description specificity as judged by an LLM, not about shot decomposition, and not about a fixed video model's output.
> 3. **ViMax "PC" is the Event-Aware Process Chain, not candidate selection**, and its removal hits one column only (NF, faithfulness to the novel, 3.52 → 2.34). **ViMax "RAG" retrieves the source novel/screenplay, not camera language.** The report's phrase "给镜头语言做检索增强的 RAG" is wrong. The earlier notes' "review/selection … ViMax −3.8 % or −0.24" mixed two unrelated ablations.
> 4. **ViMax's −3.8 % is keyframe selection, not take selection**, measured on an automatic ViCLIP similarity (Overall GC). In v2 the same paper shows best-of-3 and best-of-4 keyframes scoring *lower* than best-of-2 (0.5041 and 0.5194 vs 0.5571).
> 5. **Co-Director: MovieAgent and AniMaker were not on older renderers.** They were "instantiated using identical foundation models: Veo 3.1". So 3.22 and 3.07 vs raw Veo 3.1 at 3.71 means older agent *architectures* on the same renderer scored below the raw model. The report's reading ("旧模型加 agent 却比新模型不加 agent 低") must be replaced. The same table also has Kling 3.0 Omni 3.59 and Wan 2.6 3.48.
> 6. **Script paper: the script-only step is not the smallest.** Table 5 (three LLM critics, no humans) gives, averaged over five dimensions (computed here): script-only +0.06 to +0.24, adding segmentation +0.06 to +0.24, adding frame anchoring +0.10 to +0.16. The three steps are about equal. The third-party "0.1–0.2" estimate is in range, but "segmentation and anchoring carry most of it" overstates the table.
> 7. **MAViS "Narrative 34.00 → 13.67" has no stated scale.** The paper never defines the unit of Table 4. The Structure Reviewer is not a reviewer of "narrative structure": it "focuses on how scenes and shots are segmented and arranged".
> 8. **FilMaster 4.17 → 4.67 is one case** ("in Table 5 on one case"), scored by an LLM judge.
> 9. **CineTechBench: Gemini-2.5-Pro is not the best overall.** On static-technique QA (Table 1) GPT-4o is first at 70.16 % and Gemini-2.5-Pro second at 69.67 %. Gemini-2.5-Pro is best only on camera-movement QA (Table 2, 56.69 %).
> 10. **VideoStudio's "ModelScopeT2V 37.9" is the oracle variant.** In VideoDirectorGPT's own Table 3, 37.9 is "ModelScopeT2V (with GT co-reference; oracle)"; plain ModelScopeT2V is 16.3.
> 11. **PersonaShot does not use the words "micro-expression persistence"** as a finding. Its Finding 3 is "physical and affective state propagation remain major challenges"; the lowest columns are FATC (facial-action temporal coherence) and EAC (emotional arc coherence).
> 12. **VISTA's 45.9 % / 46.3 % are win rates with ties**, judged by Gemini 2.5 Flash; losses are 13.9 % and 11.2 %. The 66.4 % human figure is against the strongest *baseline* (VSR/VSR++), not against direct prompting.
> 13. **arXiv 2503.15138 is withdrawn** (v2 is a withdrawal notice by the first author; v1 HTML still loads). The VideoGen-of-Thought paper is arXiv 2412.02259 (v3, 2 Oct 2025). All other arXiv ids in the earlier notes that I opened resolve to the paper described.

## 1. Verification ledger: every number from this thread that the report uses

### Takeaway
Of the report's numbers from this thread, the values are almost all printed in the primary tables as quoted; what was wrong is what several of them measure (ViMax, Co-Director, MAViS, FilMaster, CineTechBench, the script-only step). ### Inferences
- StoryEngine gives the cleanest reading of "what does planning text alone buy": on the same renderer and the same planner LLM, a 2025-design agent (MovieAgent) lands within 0.02 of a planner-free baseline, and removing StoryEngine's own state planning costs 0.014, while removing reference/spatial grounding costs 0.057 and removing continuity-aware generation 0.044. Planning raises event completion; it does not carry anything across a cut unless it also decides the conditioning.
- VGoT and VideoStudio show the same thing from the other side: an LLM storyline without visual anchors leaves consistency flat (VGoT CS-FC 0.1129 → 0.1174) or lower in human rank (VideoStudio Table 5, section 3), and anchors without the storyline score higher on consistency metrics but, per the authors, repeat the same shot.
- FilmWorld's spread (2.42 to 4.25 on humans, one renderer) is the largest agent-layer effect in this set. It is on novel-to-film, where a large share of the score is fidelity to a long source text; it should not be transferred to a 60-second original short without that caveat.

## 3. Planner × renderer crossings, native multi-shot vs agent pipelines, and who uses Seedance / H3 / Claude

### Takeaway
Crossings now exist, though none is fully factorial with human raters: five papers run several agent systems on one fixed renderer (including H3 Turbo and Seedance 2.0), three run one agent on two renderers, and four swap the planner LLM under a fixed pipeline. Native multi-shot models have been compared with shot-by-shot pipelines in six papers; the outcome depends on the agent's design and the benchmark, and no paper compares Seedance 2.0's native multi-shot against an agent driving Seedance 2.0 one shot at a time.

### Inferences
- On H3 Turbo and on Seedance 2.0 specifically, the spread between agent designs is of the size the report assumed: on Seedance 2.0, 3.60 to 4.02 between four systems and +0.36 more from a policy evolved on earlier productions (VLM judge, 0–5); on H3 Turbo, 0.76 to 0.89 on judged cross-shot consistency and a 52–59 % human preference share for the best system among seven. Neither paper tells us what the raw model scores without an agent.
- An agent layer is not automatically a gain. Three independent tables put older agent designs at or below the bare renderer or a planner-free baseline on the same model (Co-Director human MOS; StoryEngine Avg and anchor persistence; MSVBench on a fixed plan). What separates the agents that help from those that do not is, in each case, the conditioning they construct (references, carried frames, repair), which is the report's thesis, but the report should say "a planning layer can also lose to the raw model".
- Planner LLM choice mostly changes the text. ViMax's Claude-Sonnet-4.6 row is the sharpest case: best-judged storyboards, worst rendered consistency. DirectorBench and VideoStudio say the same in prose and in an old preference table. For this project that argues against expecting a stronger planner model, by itself, to show up in the picture.
- PersonaShot's Seedance rows are the only same-model test of "write per-shot prompts vs one global prompt" on a current commercial model: per-shot prompting is better on every column but by 0.03 aggregate. That is small next to the between-model gaps in the same table (0.79 vs 0.58–0.69).
- StoryEngine's result that agent design outweighs renderer choice (0.24 vs 0.03) and the report's "模型换代挪得更多" are both true of what they measure: StoryEngine's metrics are narrative and continuity checks with no picture-quality term; Co-Director's human MOS includes visual quality. A report sentence should name which.

### Gaps
- No paper found compares Seedance 2.x native multi-shot, Kling 3.0 multi-shot or Veo 3.x against an agent that drives the *same* model one shot at a time, with human raters.
- No paper found uses MiniMax Hailuo (hosted) as the fixed renderer; H3 appears only as the LightX2V 8-step distilled "MiniMax-H3 Turbo" in CineEvolve. CineEvolve's supplementary material (metric and user-study details) was not available in the HTML.
- No paper found rates *video* produced under a Claude planner with human raters; SAGE is text-only and self-judged, ViMax's Claude row is an automatic similarity plus an LLM-judged text score.
- No fully factorial planner × renderer study with human ratings exists in what I read; StoryEngine (4 × 2, VLM judge) is the closest.

## 4. What the authors of the strongest ablation papers say matters most, and the failures they show

### Takeaway
In the four papers with the most complete ablations (StoryEngine, FilmWorld, OSOD, Co-Director), the authors each single out a component that fixes what a shot is conditioned on or what state it must respect, not a wording or reasoning component; and the failure cases they print are, almost without exception, failures of the image or video model to honour a correct plan.

### Cited Findings
- StoryEngine: "w/o GRP causes the largest drop" (grounded render planning: canonical character and scene references plus spatial grounding). Gains are attributed "to the continuity gate, which carries compatible anchors across cuts, to the canonical character and scene references, which fix recurring identities and environments, and to the evaluation-guided repair loop". Remaining failure: state progression (SPS) "remains below 0.67 for all methods, leaving irreversible state progression as an open challenge". Limitation: "Separating semantics from pixels prevents visual errors from entering the story state, but it also makes planning mistakes persistent." Baseline failure shown: ViMax "places the backpack in the locker before it is stored and omits the storage action". — [arXiv 2609.33627](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33627)
- FilmWorld: "Removing the explicit world state causes the sharpest degradation (90.15 → 84.28), with Character Consistency collapsing from 82.12 to 54.50. Without an addressable state identifier, downstream generators re-infer entity identity per shot and drift heavily". Limitations: errors in the parsed trajectory propagate, "yielding films that are internally consistent yet subtly unfaithful to the source"; discrete attributes quantize "gradual aging, evolving lighting, and subtle emotional shifts". — [arXiv 2607.19038](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19038)
- OSOD: "Removing 3D First-Frame causes the largest drop in continuity metrics, while leaving narrative scores relatively stable"; "Removing Multi-Stage Review leads to consistent degradation across all metrics"; "each component targets a distinct failure mode". Failure shown in a Seedance 2.0-based product: "the boss and employee interact across an office partition in the previous tail frame, but move to a corridor-like space in the next first frame". On reusing the previous tail frame as the next first frame (ScriptAgent): it "helps preserve local appearance continuity but limits viewpoint changes". Cost: about $17.3 per minute of video before regeneration (Kling v3 Pro I2V at $0.168/s). — [arXiv 2605.22144](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22144)
- Co-Director: keyframe refinement has "a vital role in preserving product visual identity" (−9.8 VAF without it); storyline refinement "primarily penalizes semantic metrics". Failure shown and caught by the reviewer: "Scene 1 features a younger brunette woman, while the Character Reference and Scenes 3 & 4 feature an older woman with grey hair"; the fix is re-injected into the *image* prompt. On judging: "humans viewing real-time playback often find transient diffusion artifacts less visually disruptive. The MLLM, conversely, processes frames analytically, strictly penalizing brief structural or temporal inconsistencies." — [arXiv 2604.24842](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24842)
- ViMax: the fundamental difficulty is cross-shot dependency; the "parallel" variant shows "that character portraits alone cannot sustain identity, layout, and scene state". Limitations: "the keyframe generator falters in crowded scenes with contact-rich interaction, and the video generator does not always respect keyframe semantics (e.g., a character on a treadmill animated running off it), with errors cascading downstream"; the workflow "does not yet address audio, dialogue synchronization, shot-level pacing, or interactive revision". — [arXiv 2606.07649v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07649v2)
- Script paper: the ScripterAgent is credited with "fine-grained visual control" and the DirectorAgent (segmentation plus frame anchoring) with "temporal dimensions"; remaining after anchoring: "imperfect lip synchronization and residual misalignment of fine-grained actions". Across models, "strict adherence occasionally impacts pure aesthetic scores". — [arXiv 2601.17737](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17737)
- MovieAgent, Section 8.2 failure cases: face and animation distortions; "character identities are inconsistent, particularly for small targets"; models "struggle to produce complex human interactions, especially when prompts include actions like walking and talking"; an abrupt cut where "Shot 1 shows Shenggongbao charging, but Shot 2 skips directly to Ne Zha preparing to defend"; distorted hands and limbs. Only the fourth is a planning fault. — [arXiv 2503.07314](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07314)
- MAViS, Limitations: "limited reliability of the T2I and I2V Evaluators. Current multimodal foundation models still exhibit limited accuracy in assessing visual content, resulting in suboptimal scoring performance when ranking candidate outputs"; storylines "lack interactions and dialogue between characters"; no editing stage. Its script rules are written around model limits: one simple action per shot, no fine text or small patterns, avoid consecutive shots in the same or adjacent backgrounds. — [arXiv 2508.08487](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08487)
- CoAgent, Section 8.2: "physics hallucinations" such as a hand clipping through a laptop bag; "Since CoAgent operates as a high-level orchestration layer, it cannot easily inject low-level physical constraints". — [arXiv 2512.22536](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.22536)
- Lights, Camera, Consistency: "the I2I seed frame is the most vital component for locking in character identity". — [arXiv 2512.16954](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16954)
- AniMaker, Limitations: "a significant gap remains between current model capabilities and the quality required for commercial film production"; the video models are "prone to producing animation artifacts, along with issues of incoherence and hallucination". — [arXiv 2506.10540](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10540)
- VISTA, Limitations: evaluation "relies primarily on MLLMs and automated metrics, which may introduce systematic biases"; the method "requires both MLLMs and T2V models with strong instruction-following"; gains on Veo 2 are smaller than on Veo 3. — [arXiv 2510.15831](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15831)
- DirectorBench: "improvements should focus on workflow architecture before model selection, unless the application is explicitly narrative-sensitive"; future workflows "may require explicit global-state propagation or post-generation self-revision mechanisms". — [arXiv 2605.30090](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30090)

### Inferences
- The authors' own rankings agree with each other more than the report's cross-paper inference could show: grounding and references (StoryEngine, VideoStudio, LCC), explicit entity state (FilmWorld), first-frame construction (OSOD), keyframe review (Co-Director). None names prompt phrasing or chain-of-thought as the main lever; MovieAgent's CoT toggle is worth +0.06.
- Review loops are described by their authors as a second-order corrector (FilmWorld: "residual defect corrector"; OSOD: uniform small losses; CoAgent: 28 % of shots need a retry), and several papers warn that the reviewer is the weak part (MAViS, VISTA) or biased toward calm footage (MAViS Dynamic Degree) or toward frame-level nitpicks humans do not see in motion (Co-Director).
- The printed failure cases split cleanly: plan-side failures are omissions and ordering (a missing transition, an action done before its precondition, a state that resets); model-side failures are identity at small scale, contact-rich interaction, physics, lip sync, and the video model overriding its keyframe. An agent can reduce the first group by structure; for the second it can only choose not to ask.

### Gaps
- None of these papers reports how often each failure type occurs; the cases are illustrative figures.
- No paper measures a reviewer that sees motion against one that sees only frames on the same takes.
- Authors' statements about importance are tied to their own metric sets, most of which are VLM-judged; only OSOD, FilmWorld, Co-Director, MVAgent and CineEvolve back the system ranking with human raters, and only OSOD prints an ablation whose columns were also human-validated.
